# Proof: No record yet bridges 1771–1780. The Prince Edward George Cook is not shown to be George Cook Sr. or the father of George Cook Jr. of Sandy Creek, and the Sr.–Jr. relationship is unproven.

This is a proof argument for a **stalled** question. It states what the evidence supports, grades each hypothesis,
and names the records that would settle it. It does not claim `proven`.

## Question
Do primary records place George Cook (Sr. and/or Jr.) and Electious Musick between the last Prince Edward sale (1771)
and Sandy Creek, Pittsylvania (1780–1783)? Is George Cook Jr. the son of the Prince Edward George Cook, and is George
Cook Sr. the father of George Cook Jr.? (plan.md)

## Conclusion
- **H1, Prince Edward George → Sandy Creek; Jr. is his son: `possible`.** Name, region, and order of events only.
- **H2, the Sandy Creek Georges are unrelated to the Prince Edward man: `possible`.** Open, not disproven.
- **H3, no 1771–1779 record exists: unresolved.** The groups that would test it are unread, not searched negative.
- **H4, Sr. is Jr.'s father: `possible`.** Two men styled Sr. and Jr., and land passing Jr. → Sr., with no stated relationship.

**Status: stalled (plan R3).** Every record group that could settle H1 or H2 needs a login, an interlibrary loan, or
the owner's approval to download.

## Evidence
1. **Prince Edward, 1768–1771.**
   - On 18 Apr 1768 George Cook acknowledged a deed to Alexander Guill in Prince Edward court
     [[S-pe-ob4-p102-guill-deed-acknowledged-1768]].
   - On 6 Apr 1771 George Cook "of the County of Prince Edward" sold 250 ac on the head branches of Harrisses Creek to
     Lovel Wil[k?]erson for £30. The land was "all that tract ... he ... now Possesses and lives on". The deed was proved
     by witnesses in June and on 16 Sep 1771 [[S-pe-db5-p67-68-cook-to-wilkerson-1771]].
   - The deed names no wife, heir, or destination. *Inference:* selling his home tract fits a departure, but the deed
     does not say he left.
2. **Musick on Sandy Creek, 1772.** The LVA index records a patent of 1 Aug 1772 to Electious Musick for 194 ac on
   Russell Creek of Sandy Creek, adjoining George Musick [[S-va-land-patents40-p861-musick-1772-index]]. A 1780 grant
   adjoins "Musick" on Sandy Creek [[S-va-land-grants-a-p425-stamps-adjoins-musick-1780-index]]. Musick was there
   within the gap. Nothing in these records mentions Cook.
3. **1767 and 1777 lists.** No George Cook appears on the 1767 tithables
   [[S-pittsylvania-tithables-1767-clement-vagenweb-transcription]] or the 1777 oath transcription
   [[S-pittsylvania-oath-of-allegiance-1777-vagenweb-transcription]]. Both are third-remove transcriptions with known
   omissions. Their silence is weak negative evidence.
4. **Sandy Creek, 1780.** On 2 Mar 1780 Electious Musick Senior sold 97 ac on a branch of Sandy Creek to "George Cooke
   Junior of the County of Pittsylvania" [[S-pittsylvania-db6-p34-35-musick-to-cooke-1780]]. This is the earliest
   Q-15 record of Jr. He was already a county resident. *Inference:* "Junior" implies an older George Cook in the
   community. It does not by itself imply father and son.
5. **Two Georges, 1782–1785.**
   - The 1782 enumeration lists "Cook, George, jr", "Cook, George, Senr", and "Cook, Elijah"
     [[S-va-heads-of-families-1782-pittsylvania-p41-cook]].
   - The 1782 land tax lists George Cook Jr with 100 ac and no Sr. [[S-pitt-land-tax-1782-vagenweb-cook-jr-100ac]].
   - The 1785 enumeration lists George Cook jr and George Cook [[S-va-heads-of-families-1785-pittsylvania-p100-cook]].
   - At least two adult George Cooks lived in the county: probable.
6. **Jr. to Sr., 1783.** On 9 Aug 1783 George Cook Junr. "and Ruth his Wife" sold about 100 ac on Sandy Creek,
   "part of the same Tract the said George Cook Junr. purchased of Electus Musick", to George Cook Senor. for £25.
   Only Jr. signed. No relationship is stated [[S-pittsylvania-db7-p106-107-cook-jr-to-cook-sr-1783]]. The 1783 land
   book records the transfer [[S-pitt-land-alterations-1783-vagenweb]], but it derives from the deed and is not
   independent.

**Testing the hypotheses.**
- **H1.** The evidence for H1 is that a George Cook left (or at least sold out of) Prince Edward in 1771 and two
  George Cooks were on Sandy Creek by 1780–1782. That is name, region, and sequence. No record shares a wife, tract,
  associate, or "late of" style between the clusters (analysis §4, C4). *Inference:* a man established in Prince Edward
  by 1768 would more naturally be the Senior than the Junior of a community he joined. That favors, if anything,
  PE = SR, which H1 does not require. `possible`.
- **H2.** Nothing contradicts H2. Several Cook households lived in Pittsylvania in 1767–1785 (William, Zacheriah, John,
  Joseph, Josiah, Harmon, Benjamin [[S-pitt-land-tax-1782-vagenweb-cook-jr-100ac]],
  [[S-pittsylvania-oath-of-allegiance-1777-vagenweb-transcription]]), so the surname alone carries no weight.
  `possible`, open.
- **H3.** Silence from the 1767 and 1777 transcriptions cannot stand for groups 3–6, which were not read. Unresolved.
- **H4.** For H4: the Sr./Jr. styles, the transfer of Jr.'s whole holding to a landless Sr. (C5), and Elijah Cook's
  presence as a witness and as the household next to Sr. Against certainty: `era-early-modern` warns that Jr./Sr.
  means older and younger same-name men, not necessarily father and son. The deed is a sale, not a gift. The price
  difference (£75 in 1780 vs. £25 in 1783) is explained by currency depreciation and is not a family discount
  (inference). `possible`.

## Limits
Any of the following would move H1 or H4 to `probable` or overturn them:
- A Pittsylvania or Prince Edward record that styles George Cook "late of Prince Edward" or "of Pittsylvania".
- A tithable series showing one George Cook from 1772 joined by a second, younger George.
- Any record naming Jr. as Sr.'s son, or as the son of another man.

The 1782 white-soul counts, the last digit of the DB 7 court year (it reads 1783 but the digit touches the scan shadow), and the film and frame provenance of both deed images are unverified.

## Addendum: Pittsylvania grantor and grantee indexes, letters C and M (group 9)
Downloaded 2026-11-28 from the Internet Archive scans of the clerk's indexes (1765–1889) and read from the images. These are indexes (derivative; they give recording dates, not deed dates), not the deeds.
- **No George Cook as grantor or grantee in Pittsylvania from 1768 to 1779.** Grantor C lists only George Jr. and Ruth to George Cook Sr., recorded 19 Aug 1783, DB 7:106 [[S-pitt-grantor-index-c-p26-cook-1769-1773]], [[S-pitt-grantor-index-c-p27-cook-1773-1814]]. Grantee C lists only DB 6:34 (1780) and DB 7:106 (1783) for a George [[S-pitt-grantee-index-cook-p24-1768-1794]], [[S-pitt-grantee-index-cooke-p29-1780-1810]]. Other Cooks (Harmon, John, William, Joseph, Shem, Benjamin) have many entries.
- **Elijah Cook:** one grantee entry, from Peter Martin, 20 Jun 1785, DB 7:453. No Elisha as a grantee in range, no Zachariah.
- **Musick:** Electious Musick Sr. sells to George Cooke Jr. (DB 6:34, recorded 15 Aug 1780) and "Elexicous/Elixicous" Musick sells to John Johnson, DB 6:93, recorded 18 Apr 1781 [[S-pitt-grantor-index-m-p36-musick-1780-1781]]. No Musick as a grantee (he held land by patent). The 1781 given-name cell is typed on two lines and is not a settled reading.
- **Effect:** H3 is partly tested for Pittsylvania land: no land deed in the county names a George Cook before the 1780 purchase, so George Cook Jr. did not buy or sell recorded Pittsylvania land earlier. This does not cover Pittsylvania order books, tithables, Lunenburg, Halifax or North Carolina. It slightly favors a late arrival (about 1779–1780) and does not change H1, H2 or H4: all stay `possible`. The question stays stalled.
- **Limits:** index pages were mostly read at 40–110 dpi, some page ranges were skimmed, and the indexes list only the first grantee or grantor of a multi-party deed.
- **New pulls:** DB 7:453 (Elijah Cook, 1785), DB 6:93 (Musick to Johnson, 1781), DB 6:34 recording endorsement, DB 5:208 (attorney power, grantor name unclear).

## Addendum 2: free Internet Archive deed-book, oath, vestry and survey scans (group 10)
Read from the clerk's page images, 2026-11-28 (logs `group-10-*.md`).
- **Elijah Cook, DB 7:453–454.** Peter Martin of Halifax County sold Elijah Cook of Pittsylvania 50 acres on Sandy Creek "where Elijah Cook now lives", 4 Jun 1785 (proved 20 Jun 1785). One boundary runs "to George Cooks (Senr) corner pine", and "George Cook Sener" witnesses [[S-pittsylvania-db7-p453-454-martin-to-elijah-cook-1785]]. So George Cook Sr. still held Sandy Creek land (or at least a corner) in 1785, which gives H4 and the Sr. cluster slightly more weight. No relationship is stated.
- **DB 6:93.** "Elixeous [?] Musick of Henry County" sold 97 acres on Sandy Creek to John Johnson of Cumberland County, made 29 Jul 1780 for £2000, recorded 17 Apr 1781 (the index said 18 Apr) [[S-pittsylvania-db6-p93-musick-to-johnson-1780]]. This is the same 97-acre figure as Musick's 2 Mar 1780 sale to George Cooke Jr. with different bounds, so it is not known whether these are one tract or two. The DB 6:34–35 recording date, 15 Aug 1780, is confirmed. Musick was "of Henry County" by July 1780, which fits a man leaving the area.
- **1777 oath, IA original.** The fair copy has no George, Elijah, Elisha or Zachariah Cook and no Electious Musick; a Thomas Musick appears (sheet 284). The "Geo. -" fragment is one of three unfinished first names with no surname, so it is not a lead [[S-pittsylvania-oath-of-allegiance-1777-ia-1777oath-fair-copy]]. It is a fair copy of a fair copy, so the negative stays weak.
- **Camden Parish vestry book, folios 1–49a (1767–1785).** No George, Elijah, Elisha, Musick, Flippin, Echols or Collie. John, Joseph, William and Benjamin Cook appear as collectors, sureties and processioners on the northern waters (Pigg River, Snow Creek). The three Sandy Creek processionings (1771, 1776, 1780) name no Cook, but they are for the county-line Sandy Creek and are not tied to the DB 6 tract.
- **Surveys, claims, marriage bonds:** a weak negative, partly unread (see log).
- **Effect:** H1, H2 and H4 stay `possible`; H3 for Pittsylvania land, 1768–1779, is now supported by both indexes and the DB 5 pages read, but not for order books, tithables, Lunenburg, Halifax or North Carolina. The question stays stalled.
