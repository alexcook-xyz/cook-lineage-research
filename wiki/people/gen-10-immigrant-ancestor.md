# Generation 10+: Transatlantic Immigrant Ancestor

- **Lineage Position:** Earliest Colonial Immigrant Ancestor
- **Lineage Proof Status:** **Unidentified / Under Active Investigation**
- **Target Era:** 17th or early 18th century (pre-1750).
- **Primary Arrival Vectors:** Port of London, Bristol, or Liverpool → Chesapeake Bay / James River Basin / Tidewater Virginia or Maryland.

---

## 1. Research Scope & Problem Statement

To trace the patrilineal line of Alex Cook from the 1750 Piedmont patentee (George Cook of Harris's Creek) back to the original colonial immigrant ancestor who crossed the Atlantic from the British Isles.

---

## 2. Systematic Search Strategy

1. **Colonial Headright Patents (1623–1749):**
   - Systematically extract all Cook individuals who imported persons or were transported as headrights in Virginia Land Patent Books 1 through 28 (tracked in Issue #11).
   - Focus specifically on the Southside waterways: Appomattox River, Blackwater River, Nottoway River, and James River (counties of Prince George, Surry, Isle of Wight, and Charles City).
2. **British Emigration & Port Registers:**
   - Correlate Virginia patent dates against Peter Wilson Coldham's *The Complete Book of Emigrants (1607–1776)* and *Emigrants in Chains* (tracked in Issue #12).
   - Check Bristol and London apprentice registers for Cook youths bound to Virginia planters.
3. **Prince George County Parent Records:**
   - Amelia County was created in 1734 from Prince George and Brunswick counties. Surviving Prince George court orders and deeds (1710–1740) must be scoured for the father of the 1750 patentee.

---

## 3. Exclusion of Spurious Published Claims

Commercial internet family trees frequently attach arbitrary English origins (e.g., claiming Captain James Cook or random London merchants). Under our acceptance standards, no European origin will be accepted without direct documentary chain of title or court-tested probate proof linking the immigrant to the Southside Virginia line.
