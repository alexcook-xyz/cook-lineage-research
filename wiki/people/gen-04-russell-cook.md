## Biographical Overview & Life

**Russell Cook** (28 November 1916 – 8 January 1982) was born and raised in Adair County, Kentucky, on the eve of American entry into World War I. Born to George William Cook, an established farmer in his sixties, and teenage bride Emma Janes, Russell was reared along Russell's Creek in an environment steeped in traditional subsistence farming and timber trades. 

As a young man during the Great Depression, Cook moved into the town of Columbia to seek commercial employment. By October 1940, at the age of twenty-three, he registered in the initial national peacetime draft for World War II, reporting his occupation at Goff's Furniture Store on Public Square in Columbia and naming his mother, Mrs. Anna Cook, as his nearest relative. Respected locally for his steady work ethic and deep family ties throughout the county, Cook lived through the post-war modernization of rural South-Central Kentucky. He died in Columbia in January 1982 at the age of sixty-five and was interred at Cook Cemetery (Find a Grave Memorial #96831073).
