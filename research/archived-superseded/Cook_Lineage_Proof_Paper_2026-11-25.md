# The Cook Line of Alex Haley Cook

## A Proof Argument from Alex Haley Cook to George Cook of Pittsylvania County, Virginia

Prepared for Alex Haley Cook · 25 November 2026

**Standard applied:** the Genealogical Proof Standard (GPS) of the Board for Certification of Genealogists — (1) reasonably exhaustive research, (2) complete and accurate source citations, (3) analysis and correlation of the evidence, (4) resolution of conflicting evidence, and (5) a soundly reasoned, coherently written conclusion.[^1] Citations follow the *Chicago Manual of Style* (notes and bibliography), modeled on Elizabeth Shown Mills, *Evidence Explained*. The project's 0–100 link scores are a working tool under the GPS, not a substitute for it; a link is called **proven** here only where the evidence satisfies all five GPS elements.

**A note on the surname.** No deliberate change from "Cooke" to "Cook" occurred. In the eighteenth century spelling was set by clerks, not by families, and this line appears as Cook, Cooke, and Coke within three years: George Cook Jr. bought as "George Cooke Junior" in 1780, the deed index filed that purchase under "Coke," and he sold as "Cook" in 1783.[^2] All searches behind this paper were run under every variant.

**Headline result.** The six parent–child links from Alex Cook down to George B (no. 7) are **proven**. The seventh link — George B as son of the testator George Cook (no. 8), the oldest Cook on the tree — is a **strong hypothesis, scored 80 (Probable), and is not proven**. It is presented below as a hypothesis, with the evidence for and against, exactly as the GPS requires. The testator's own father has not been identified; the negative evidence is summarized at the end.

## 1. Alex Haley Cook, son of Brian Allen Cook

Alex Haley Cook was born 17 October 1991 in Hardin County, Kentucky; the Kentucky Birth Index entry names his mother as Kristi S. Hall.[^3] His father is Brian Allen Cook of Campbellsville, Kentucky. The identification rests on Alex's own firsthand knowledge — he is the subject of this research and the primary informant for his own parentage — corroborated by the family record set: Brian's brother Mike Cook's 2025 obituary names Brian Cook of Campbellsville among the brothers of the same sibling group,[^4] and Brian's parentage (section 2) anchors him in the line. No Kentucky birth certificate naming Alex's father has been obtained; Kentucky certificates of this era are not public records. The link is accepted on direct personal knowledge with record corroboration.

## 2. Brian Allen Cook, son of Arlin Cook

Brian Allen Cook was born 12 July 1962, per the Kentucky Birth Index, and lives at Campbellsville, Taylor County.[^5] That he is the son of Arlin Cook and Elsie Mae (Hunt) Cook is stated by Alex from family knowledge received from his father, and is corroborated by the documented sibling set: Mike Cook's obituary names Mike (1958–2025), Arlin Ray Cook, and Brian Cook as brothers, all of Campbellsville,[^4] and Elsie Cook's 1986 obituary names Brian's son Jonathan among her grandsons. The documentary record for this generation is thinner than for earlier ones — mid-twentieth-century Kentucky birth certificates are closed — and the link is carried by consistent family testimony from two independent directions (Alex's statement and the published obituaries), with no conflicting evidence of any kind.

## 3. Arlin Cook, son of Russell Cook — Proven (score 88)

Arlin Cook was born 24 August 1935 in Adair County and died there 15 December 1991.[^6] His parentage is directly proven. His Social Security NUMIDENT record — the data behind his Social Security application — names his father as **Russel Cook** and his mother as **Anna B. Sneed**.[^7] The Kentucky Birth Index independently records his birth (indexed under the variant "Harlin Cook") on the same date in Adair County, naming his mother as Annie Sneed.[^8] The census record places him where the documents predict: in 1940, age five, in his mother's Adair County household with his sister Hazel Mae,[^9] and in 1950, age fourteen, in the household of his by-then-divorced father Russell.[^10] Three independent record systems — Social Security, state vital records, and the federal census — agree. No conflict exists.

## 4. Russell Cook, son of George William Cook — Proven (score 86)

Russell Cook, a painter of Columbia, Adair County, was born 28 November 1916 and died 8 January 1982.[^11] His 1916 Kentucky birth index entry names his mother as "Anna Jeans" — the bride of the 1911 marriage record below, Emma Anne Janes, whose name the records render variously as Enna, Anna, and Emma Anne Janes.[^12] The 1920 and 1930 censuses show Russell as the young son in the Adair County household of George Cook and his wife Anna.[^13] The relationship is confirmed from the sibling side: Russell's younger brother James Robert Cook died at Shelbyville, Indiana, in 1949, and his obituary names his parents as **George and Anna (Janes) Cook** of Columbia and Russell among his surviving brothers;[^14] James Robert's Indiana death certificate likewise names his father as George Cook and his mother as Anna Janes.[^15] Census, vital, and newspaper evidence correlate without material conflict.

## 5. George William Cook, son of George W. Cook — Proven (the "bridge," score 90)

George William Cook was born 26 February 1852 and died in Adair County on 16 July 1935, of intestinal tuberculosis; he is buried in Cook Cemetery on Russell Creek.[^16] His death certificate names his father as George Cook and his mother as a Cole.[^16] He married Emma Anne Janes in Adair County on 27 September 1911 (bond dated 26 September), the license expressly recording it as the groom's **first marriage**, at age fifty-nine.[^17]

The proof that he is the son of George W. Cook (no. 6) and Eliza E. Cole — the link the project calls the bridge — is a four-record chain:

1. **The 1877 deed.** On 17 February 1877 George W. Cook conveyed his inherited share of land to his brother Thomas H. Cook, describing it in his own words as land "owned by my father George Cook at the time of his death," the share having "descended to me as heir at law of my father."[^18] This is direct evidence of the father–son relationship from the son himself.
2. **Eliza's will.** Eliza E. Cook's will, dated 1 October 1890 and probated 1 January 1906, names "my two sons Thomas H. Cook and Geo. W. Cook," leaving George W. the remainder, including her interest in the land she lived on.[^19]
3. **The 1874 deed.** A deed of 30 May 1874 styles Thomas H. Cook as "son & heir of Geo. Cook decd" and Eliza E. as "widow of Geo. Cook decd," tying the same family group together a generation up.[^20]
4. **The 1900 census.** George W. Cook, born February 1852 and still single, appears as son in the widowed Eliza's District 7, Columbia, household — matching, in age, status, and place, the man whose 1911 license declares a first marriage to Emma Janes.[^21] The 1860 and 1870 censuses show the same child in George and Eliza's household.[^22]

**Conflict and resolution.** George William's death certificate gives his father's birthplace as Kentucky; the 1860 census gives George W. Cook's birthplace as Pittsylvania County, Virginia. The certificate's informant was his widow Emma, speaking of a father-in-law who died four decades before her marriage into the family — secondary information at best — while the census statement was made in George W.'s own lifetime and is corroborated by the Pittsylvania deed chain in section 7. The conflict is resolved in favor of Virginia. Ancestry's index to the 1935 certificate is additionally corrupt (it lists his mother's maiden name as his spouse); the certificate image, not the index, was used.

## 6. George W. Cook, son of George B — Proven (score 92)

George W. Cook was born about 1813 in Virginia. He married Eliza E. Cole on 1 January 1844,[^23] was in Kentucky by about 1845 — the 1850 census of Cumberland County shows George W. Cook, age 37, born in Virginia, with his son Thomas, age 5, born in Kentucky[^24] — first appears in the Cumberland County tax lists in 1848, bought 167¾ acres on Russell Creek, Adair County, in 1866,[^25] and died before September 1873, when a deed already speaks of "George Cook's heirs."[^26] His 1860 census entry gives his birthplace, in the only direct statement found, as "Pitsylvania Va."[^27]

His father is identified by the settlement of a Pittsylvania County estate:

- On **15 October 1831**, Catharine Cook, widow, joined with the children **David, James, Elizabeth, John, Washington, Henry, and George** Cook to sell 277 acres to James Nance. The two youngest, Henry and George, were minors and did not sign.[^28]
- On **2 November 1836** the younger heirs executed a power of attorney, and on **10 November 1836** James Cook — by then **James Cook of Adair County, Kentucky** — conveyed the minors' shares to Nance, the papers reciting that George and Henry "were infants" at the 1831 sale.[^29]

The correlation is exact. A George Cook who was a minor in October 1831 and an infant heir of a Pittsylvania father was born in the 1810s; George W. Cook's census age gives about 1813. His brother James was of Adair County by 1836 — the county where George W. himself settled, bought his Russell Creek land, and died — and the 1860 census preserves George W.'s own statement that he was born in Pittsylvania County. The sibling set, the ages, the Virginia origin, and the shared Kentucky destination all agree, and no second George Cook of the right age exists in the Pittsylvania records to compete for the identification. The link is proven.

**His mother is deliberately left blank.** Catharine is documented as George B's widow, but no record states she was George W.'s mother individually — and if the hypothesis in section 7 is correct, George B's earlier wife Ruth, documented as living in July 1814, could as easily be the mother of a child born about 1813. Under the GPS, an unproven maternity is not filled in.

## 7. George B of Pittsylvania County, and the Hypothesis That He Was George Cook Jr.

George B — the research label for the George Cook who died before 15 October 1831 leaving widow Catharine and the seven children above — is known almost entirely from his estate's paper trail: the 1831 sale of his 277 acres and its 1836 completion.[^28][^29] No will for him has been found, consistent with the heirs selling as heirs at law. How he acquired the 277 acres cannot be fully reconstructed: the December 1815 deed from Solomon Seal that might supply the largest component was never recorded — only Rhody Seal's dower certificate survives[^30] — and the recorded purchases under the name George Cook (1794–1815) cannot yet be assigned among the county's several George Cooks with certainty.

**The hypothesis.** George B was the **George Cook Jr.** who:

- bought 97 acres on a branch of Sandy Creek from Electus Musick Sr. on 2 March 1780, as "George Cooke Junior";[^31]
- sold 100 acres to **George Cook Senr** on 9 August 1783 for £25, the deed witnessed by Joseph Flippen and Elijah Cook;[^32]
- was taxed on 100 acres in 1782, heading his own household, putting his birth by about 1761;
- appears with wife **Ruth** in deeds of 17 July 1812 and 9 July 1814 — the latter image-verified, Ruth signing by mark;[^33]
- bought further land as George Cook Jr. on 2 August 1815.[^34]

Under this reading, Catharine was a later wife, married after July 1814 — the marriage would fall in the 1806–1830 Pittsylvania marriage records, which survive only in print and have not yet been read — and George B died between August 1815 and October 1831, possibly before June 1819, when a Catharine Cook begins conveying land in her own name.[^35]

**Evidence for:** the generational arithmetic works (a man born by ~1761 fathers a child about 1813 in his early fifties, and dies before 1831 in his late sixties); the Sandy Creek neighborhood is constant across the Jr. records and the testator's tract; Jr. disappears from the records after 1815, where George B's estate trail begins; and no competing candidate for George B's identity has survived the deed-index sweep of 1767–1836. **Evidence missing:** no record states that George Jr. and George B are the same man, and no record names George B's father. GPS element 1 (reasonably exhaustive research) is not yet met — Catharine's four deeds in her own name (1819–1824), which may recite her husband or her land's source, remain unread, and the print-only marriage volume is unchecked. The link therefore stands at **80, Probable — a hypothesis, not a proof.**

## 8. George Cook the Testator, the Oldest Cook on the Tree

If the hypothesis holds, George B's father was the George Cook called "Senr" in 1783 — the testator, and the oldest Cook on the tree. What is documented of him, independent of the hypothesis:

He was born about 1740 — his presumed son was an adult householder taxed on 100 acres by 1782. He appears in Pittsylvania County by about 1767–1770 as a neighbor in others' deeds, headed a household of seven white souls in the 1782 enumeration while owning no land,[^36] bought the 100-acre Sandy Creek tract from George Jr. in 1783,[^32] and took a 44-acre patent on Upper Double Creek on 30 March 1785.[^37] His will, dated 19 March 1827 and probated in the fall of 1835, gave his wife **Sarah** a life estate in the 97 acres on Sandy Creek "whereon I now live," with the land then to be sold and the estate divided among "all of my Children" — none named.[^38] Sarah died soon after; on 27 January 1837 the sheriff sold the 97 acres to John Williams exactly as the will directed.[^39]

**The 97 acres are the quiet centerpiece of the hypothesis:** the tract the testator died on is, in size and location, the same Sandy Creek land George Jr. bought from Musick in 1780 and conveyed to "Senr" in 1783 — and Joseph Flippen, a witness to that 1783 deed, is the man the testator named as his executor fifty years later. In eighteenth-century usage "Jr." and "Sr." most often, though not invariably, denote father and son. The correlation is strong; it is not yet proof, because the 1783 deed contains no relationship language and the will names no child.

**Disambiguation.** Three George Cooks died in Pittsylvania County within about four years, and separating them is what makes the present line defensible: George B (d. before Oct 1831, widow Catharine, 277 acres); the Earp plaintiff (d. late 1831, whose will of 17 September 1831 left everything to his daughter Sally Cook and revoked all prior wills — he cannot be the testator, whose 1827 will was probated in 1835);[^40] and the testator himself (d. 1835, wife Sarah, 97 acres). A different widow, a different death date, and a different tract attach to each man; the earlier conclusion (24 November 2026) that merged them was withdrawn the next day when the originals were read.

## 9. Beyond the Testator: Negative Evidence

The testator's father has not been identified, and the search is documented to a standard that makes the negative result itself evidence. Ruled out on documents: **William Cook Jr.** of Pigg River, whose eleven children are documented in W. G. Scroggins's study, with no George among them;[^41] **Harmon Cook** (1810 will, children named, no George); **Edward Cook** (d. 1799; estate names six children, no George — Jesse and Wesley Cook belong to Edward's family, not this one); **John Cook** of Halifax (heirs all named, no George); and **William Cook** of Charlotte County and Surry County, North Carolina (1801 will, eleven children, no George). Pittsylvania's probate books 1–2 (1770–1797) contain no Cook at all, and full-text searches of Halifax County's will books (1753–1792) and deed books (1752–1780) return "George Cook" zero times. The remaining untried records are in-person only: the Halifax general indexes and 1780–1785 deeds, the Pittsylvania land tax lists for 1786–1802, and the printed Pittsylvania will abstracts and marriage volumes at WKU Special Collections — the last of which may also settle the George B hypothesis, since a George Cook–Catharine marriage bond of about 1815–1819 would fall in *Marriages of Pittsylvania County, 1806–1830*.

## Conclusion

The Cook line of Alex Haley Cook is proven, link by link and under the Genealogical Proof Standard, from Alex through Brian Allen Cook, Arlin Cook, Russell Cook, George William Cook, and George W. Cook, to **George B of Pittsylvania County, who died before 15 October 1831**. One generation further, the testator **George Cook (about 1740–1835)** — the oldest Cook on the tree — stands as George B's probable father on the strength of the 1783 Senr/Junr deed, the identity of the 97-acre Sandy Creek tract, and fifty years of association between the same small circle of men. That final link is labeled a hypothesis wherever it appears in the family's trees, and the two record groups most likely to prove or break it — Catharine Cook's own deeds and the printed 1806–1830 Pittsylvania marriages — are identified and queued. The immigrant ancestor has not yet been reached; the honest frontier of this line, as of 25 November 2026, is a landless householder on Sandy Creek in 1782, whose father the surviving records have not yet named.
