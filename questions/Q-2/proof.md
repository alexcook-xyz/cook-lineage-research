# Q-2 Proof — George William Cook's household, Adair County, 1880–1930

## Conclusion

George William Cook (1852–1935) was present at every enumeration from 1880 to 1930 in the same part of Adair County, Kentucky — Columbia (ED 1) in 1880, then West Columbia, Magisterial District 7, in 1900, 1910, 1920 and 1930: in his mother Eliza E. Cook's household in 1880 and 1900, and as head of his own household in the same district in 1910 (alone), 1920 (with his wife Anna and son Russell), and 1930 (with Anna and sons Russell and James). That he remained on the family's Russell's Creek home tract itself is a probable inference from the will and deed records (see ¶2), not something the censuses alone establish. His brothers Thomas H. Cook and John D. Cook — the two other sons named in Eliza's will — were **not in Adair County in any of the five censuses**; their Adair households do not exist to be retrieved, and that absence is established by county-wide Cook/Cooke surveys for every year, not by a failed name search.

## Argument

1. **Identity of the George in each census.** The 1880 George (28, son of the widowed Eliza, Columbia ED 1) and the 1900 George W. (48, son of Eliza E., West Columbia) are the same man by name, age progression, relation, and place. The 1910, 1920 and 1930 heads chain to him by age (57 → 67 → 78 against b. 26 Feb 1852), bachelor status until after 1910 then marriage, farmer/owner status, unbroken West Columbia / MD 7 residence, and — in 1930 — an age at first marriage (60) that matches the documented 27 Sep 1911 marriage to Emma (Anna) Janes, whose own age at first marriage (17) matches the same record (her census ages drift by up to two years; analysis C8). Russell's sheet ages (3 1/12 in Jan 1920; 13 in May 1930) fit his 28 Nov 1916 birth exactly. Two possible confusions are distinguished, not ignored: the 1930 head is recorded as plain "George Cook" with no middle initial — the identification there rests on age, wife, sons, district, and age at first marriage, and is stated on those terms; and the surveys hold one other adult George — **George L. Cook, b. 1871, of East Columbia** (1910 survey; analysis C7) — a different man by initial, by 19 years of age, and by precinct. The remaining Georges (b. 1895, 1896/1898) are a generation down.

2. **Continuity — district proven, tract inferred.** What the censuses establish is residence in the same district at all five enumerations, farming each time, owning in 1910, 1920 and 1930. Eliza's will (8 Oct 1900, proved 1 Jan 1906) devised George "my interest in the tract of land I now live on," and the 1877 Deed Book Z transactions (repo gen-05) place the Russell's Creek tract in the family's hands a generation earlier. Reading the censuses together with those records, the probable reading is that George stayed on that tract; but no census identifies a parcel — farm schedule numbers (8 [?] in 1900, 49 in 1910, 69 in 1920) are sequence numbers in the agricultural schedules, and the neighbouring households change completely at every census — so tract-level continuity is recorded as a **probable inference**, and district-level continuity as the proven fact.

3. **The brothers' absence in Adair.** 1880 rests on the complete Adair surname index (Cook/Cooke, 30 Adair persons) plus dedicated name searches. 1900 rests on a relevance-ranked residence search whose entire 436-result set was inspected (M-2b: results 1–300; M-2c: 301–436, zero Adair) plus the M-2 named searches — 25 Adair persons, no Thomas, no John. 1910, 1920 and 1930 rest on collection-filtered surveys in which every result was inspected and accounted for (23, 14 and 8 Adair persons respectively), plus dedicated John searches in 1910 (statewide, b. 1840–1860, all 78 scanned) and 1920. A Thomas or John of the brothers' generation (b. 1840s–1850s, per prior repo work) appears in none of them. Stated limits: the negatives are index-based, covering the Cook/Cooke spellings; no Coke/Cooks variant survey, no given-name-only search, and no visual browse of the 1880 ED 2 sheets was run. Five concordant yearly surveys plus dedicated name searches are a solid GPS negative on those terms. The 1890 census is destroyed and is treated as a gap, not evidence.

4. **Conflicts are resolved, not ignored.** C1 (John D. vs M.) is resolved from the will image itself: John **D.** — a point of correctness for the repo's records; nothing in this conclusion turned on it, since no John of the right generation was found under either initial. C2 (father's birthplace Ky vs Va) is resolved for Virginia on preponderance, with the 3–3 split and the weighing stated openly in analysis.md. C3–C5 and C8 are age/literacy noise that does not touch any identification. C6 (defective repo will plates) is documented and the true plates supplied. C7 holds every unassigned Cook household unmerged.
