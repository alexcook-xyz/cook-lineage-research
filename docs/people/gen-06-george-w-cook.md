## Biographical Overview & Life

**George W. Cook** (circa 1813 – by September 1873) was the pioneer patriarch who transplanted this branch of the Cook patrilineage from the tobacco plantation lands of [Pittsylvania County, Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsylvania_County,_Virginia "Virginia's largest county by land area, formed in 1767 from Halifax County along the Dan River borderlands with its seat at Chatham."), across the Cumberland Gap into South-Central Kentucky. Born in Virginia during the War of 1812 to George Cook and his wife Catharine, George W. lost his father while still a minor. In October 1831, when his widowed mother and adult siblings conveyed their father's 277-acre Dan River/Sandy Creek estate in Pittsylvania County to James Nance, George and his younger brother Henry were minors and unable to execute legal deeds.

Seeking new opportunities in the western territories, George and Henry moved westward into [Cumberland County, Kentucky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cumberland_County,_Kentucky "Southern Kentucky county in the Pennyroyal Plateau formed in 1798 along the Cumberland River, with its county seat at Burkesville."), in the early 1830s. Upon attaining their legal majority in November 1836, the two brothers executed a power of attorney in Cumberland County authorizing their elder brother, James Cook—who had already established a homestead in neighboring [Adair County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adair_County,_Kentucky "South-central Kentucky county organized in 1801–1802 from Green County and named for John Adair, with its county seat at Columbia.")—to convey their shares in their late father's Virginia estate. By 1850, George had relocated permanently to the rich bottomlands of Russell's Creek in Adair County and married Eliza E. Cole. 

A prosperous yeoman farmer, Cook established a 142-acre homestead that became the multi-generational family seat. In both the 1860 and 1870 federal censuses, he proudly stated his birthplace as Virginia (explicitly recording *"Pittsylvania Va"* in 1860). Cook died intestate by September 1873, leaving his widow Eliza and his sons to manage the 142-acre Russell's Creek estate that would sustain his descendants into the twentieth century.

## Primary Records & Archival Plates

1. **1860 Federal Census (Adair County, KY, p. 200, dw. 727):**
   - Head of household: `George W. Cook`, age 47, farmer, born **`PITTSYLVANIA VA`**. Wife `Eliza`, age 32, born KY; son `George W.`, age 8.
   - Sibling correlation: Brother `James Cook`, age 56, born `PITTSYLVANIA VA` living nearby.
2. **1870 Federal Census (Adair County, KY, p. 11B, dw. 39):**
   - Head of household: `George Cook`, age 58, farmer, born `VIRGINIA`. Wife Eliza age 42, son George W. age 18.
3. **1836 Cumberland County, Kentucky Power of Attorney (Pittsylvania DB 39:242):**
   - Preserved in `sources/va-pittsylvania/Pittsylvania_DB39_p242.png` (Plate 9 in paper).
   - Dated 2 Nov 1836: `George Cook` and `Henry Cook` of Cumberland Co., KY empower *"our Brother James Cook of Adair County"* to convey their shares in 227/277 acres in Pittsylvania County, VA descending to them *"as heirs at law of George Cook Deceased"*.
4. **1836 Pittsylvania County Follow-up Deed (DB 39:256–257):**
   - Preserved in `sources/va-pittsylvania/Pittsylvania_DB39_p256-257.png` (Plate 10 in paper).
   - James Cook conveys to James Nance; recites that when the 1831 deed was made, George and Henry Cook *"were infants"*.
5. **1877 Adair County Heirship Deed (DB Z:195):**
   - Preserved in `sources/ky-adair/Adair_DBZ_p194-195_EXACT.png` (Plate 5 in paper).
   - Recites 142 acres owned by father George Cook at the time of his death.
