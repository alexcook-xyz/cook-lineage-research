# [Spotsylvania County, Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spotsylvania_County,_Virginia "Central Virginia county founded in 1721 and named for colonial governor Alexander Spotswood, with its seat at Spotsylvania Courthouse."): Pre-1750 Archival Guide

- **Formation:** Created 1720/1721 from Essex, King and Queen, and King William counties in the [Colony of Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colony_of_Virginia "First permanent English colony in North America (founded 1607 at Jamestown), governed by the British Crown until 1776.").
- **Parish:** St. George's Parish.
- **Key Pre-1750 Subject:** **George Cook** and his wife **Sarah** (documented 1741–1748).

---

## 1. Relevance to the Lineage

Outside of a 1645 reference in Warwick County, Spotsylvania County holds the **only verified pre-1750 `George Cook` landholder in Virginia** prior to our 3 November 1750 [Amelia County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amelia_County,_Virginia "Central Virginia county formed in 1735 from Prince George and Brunswick counties; originally included modern Prince Edward County.") patent (*Patents 29:316–317*):

1. **1741–1742 Land Acquisitions (*Crozier, pp. 158–159*; *Spotsylvania OB, pp. 166–167*):**
   - `15 May 1741 / 4 May 1742`: Bought 100–200 acres from George & Catherine Stubblefield (Harry Beverley tract).
   - `4 May 1742`: Bought 200 acres from John & Anna Sartin.
   - `12 Feb 1742[/43]` (*Patents 21:145–146*): Patented **400 acres** in Spotsylvania by **40 shillings** treasury right (West side of new Mine Bank Road adj. Griffin Fantleroy & Thomas Shelton).
2. **1743–1744 Land Liquidation (*Spotsylvania OB, p. 236*; *Crozier, pp. 164, 168*):**
   - `6 Sep 1743`: **George Cook and Sarah his wife** sold 200 acres to Rev. James Marye and 400 acres to Henry Lewis (`Spotsylvania_OB_p236-237_1743.jpg`).
   - `4 Mar 1744[/45]`: **George Cook and Sarah his wife** sold the final 200-acre Stubblefield tract to Joseph Allen of Essex County.
3. **7 June 1748 Final Pre-1750 Court Appearance (*Spotsylvania OB, pp. 466–467*):**
   - **George Cook** exhibited a certified public claim for taking up a runaway slave belonging to George Morton (`Spotsylvania_OB_p466-467_1748.jpg`).
   - Crucially, the **very next entry on page 467** is **`Charles Gordon and Elexius Musick`**. **Electious Musick Sr.** later moved south to **Sandy Creek in Pittsylvania County**, where he sold **97 acres** to **George Cooke Junior** on 2 March 1780 (*Pittsylvania DB 6:34–35*).

## 2. Why This Link Remains Under Test (~65–70% Probability)

- **For the Match:** Identical husband and wife names (**George Cook & Sarah** in Spotsylvania 1743 and Prince Edward May 1768), treasury-right land purchasing, timing (`June 1748` Spotsylvania → `Nov 1750` Amelia), and the shared **Elexius Musick** neighbor tie reaching Sandy Creek in 1780.
- **For Caution:** Between 1777 and 1783, a `George Cook` (with wife **Mary** and witness `Wm. Cook`) bought and sold ~220 acres in Spotsylvania. Because **Spotsylvania Order Book 1749–1755** has only been checked on pages 1–2 and the **General Index to Wills (1722–1947, Film 34055 / DGS 7645804)** is locked, **GitHub Issue #13** tracks verifying whether the 1741–1748 George Cook left Spotsylvania between 1748 and 1750 or stayed concurrently.
