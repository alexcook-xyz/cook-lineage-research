## Citation
"First List of Tithables of Pittsylvania County, Year 1767," *Pittsylvania County VAGenWeb* (https://www.usgenwebsites.org/vagenweb/pittsylvania/towns/tith.htm : accessed 28 Nov 2026), crediting Maud Carter Clement, *The History of Pittsylvania County, Virginia* (1929), Appendix I (not seen by this searcher; page number not given by the web copy). Underlying originals: 1767 tithable lists taken by the county's appointed gentlemen for Pittsylvania County and Camden Parish; microfilm: Library of Virginia, Pittsylvania Co., Reel 286, "Taxable Property & Census Lists, 1767-1786, undated" (LVA catalog: https://www.lva.virginia.gov/collections/ccmf/VA/VA213). Original and film NOT viewed. This is 1767, before the 1771-1782 gap in Q-15; it is logged because the plan asks for any pre-1782 tithables.

## Transcription
Entries relevant to the names sought (verbatim; each list is headed by the taker named):
- "A List of Tithables for Pittsylvania County Taken by John Wilson, Gentl, in Year 1767": "Dextrous Musick" (between "eorge Holms Gwin, negroes Lill, Lucy, Fillis & Gwin" and "William Nelson"; the first letter of "eorge" is missing in the web copy)
- "A List of Tithables Taken by Hamon Critz, Gent, for the Year 1767": "Zacheriah Cook"
- "A List of Land and Tithes Taken by Hugh Innes for Pittsylvania County Anno. Dom. 1767": "William Cook"
- "A List of Tithes, Etc, Taken by John Donelson 1767": "Thomas Mustein" (Mustain-type name, not Musick)

No entry reads George Cook, George Cooke, Elijah Cook, Electious/Elexius Musick, or "Musick" other than "Dextrous Musick". "Elijah" appears only as Elijah Farris, Elijah Harbour, and a negro "Elijah King" (a slave name in a list entry).

## Notes
- Informant: the tithable taker (a county gentleman) listing taxable males; heads of households and their taxable males, with owners listing enslaved people. Household composition is not given beyond names.
- Web copy contains visible typing artifacts (a missing first letter in "eorge", stray "]", "|", "\" characters), so it is not a clean transcription of Clement; Clement's printed text was not checked.
- "Dextrous Musick" is a possible garbled or phonetic rendering of "Electious Musick" or a different person; no evidence yet either way. Lead only; do not treat as the same man without the analyst's identity argument.
