# Q-2 Proof — George William Cook's household, Adair County, 1880–1930

## Conclusion

George William Cook (1852–1935) lived continuously on his family's home tract in the Columbia / West Columbia area of Adair County, Kentucky (Magisterial District 7) across every census from 1880 to 1930: in his mother Eliza E. Cook's household in 1880 and 1900, as head of his own household on the same ground in 1910 (alone), 1920 (with his wife Anna and son Russell), and 1930 (with Anna and sons Russell and James). His brothers Thomas H. Cook and John D. Cook — the two other sons named in Eliza's will — were **not in Adair County in any of the five censuses**; their Adair households do not exist to be retrieved, and that absence is established by county-wide Cook/Cooke surveys for every year, not by a failed name search.

## Argument

1. **Identity of the George in each census.** The 1880 George (28, son of the widowed Eliza, Columbia ED 1) and the 1900 George W. (48, son of Eliza E., West Columbia) are the same man by name, age progression, relation, and place. The 1910, 1920 and 1930 heads chain to him by age (57 → 67 → 78 against b. 26 Feb 1852), bachelor status until after 1910 then marriage, farmer/owner status, unbroken West Columbia / MD 7 residence, and — in 1930 — an age at first marriage (60) that matches the documented 27 Sep 1911 marriage to Emma (Anna) Janes, whose own age at first marriage (17) and census ages (24 in 1920, 35 in 1930) track the same woman. Russell's sheet ages (3 1/12 in Jan 1920; 13 in May 1930) fit his 28 Nov 1916 birth exactly. No competing George W. Cook of this generation appears in any Adair survey (the only other Georges are b. 1896/1898 and b. 1895, a generation down).

2. **Continuity on the home tract.** Eliza's will (8 Oct 1900, proved 1 Jan 1906) devised George "my interest in the tract of land I now live on." In 1900 he is on that tract in her household (farm schedule 8); in 1910 he heads the household alone in the same precinct (owns free, farm schedule 49); in 1920 the same, on the Greensburg Road (farm schedule 69); in 1930 the same district, "lives on a farm: Yes," farming a truck farm at 78. The 1877 Deed Book Z transactions (repo gen-05) place the same Russell's Creek tract in the family's hands a generation earlier. Five censuses, one place, one farm.

3. **The brothers' absence is exhaustive for Adair.** 1880 rests on the complete Adair surname index (Cook/Cooke, 30 Adair persons) plus dedicated name searches; 1900, 1910, 1920 and 1930 rest on collection-filtered surveys in which every result was inspected and accounted for (25, 23, 14 and 8 Adair persons respectively), plus dedicated John searches in 1910 (statewide, b. 1840–1860, all 78 scanned) and 1920. A Thomas or John of the brothers' generation (b. 1840s–1850s) appears in none of them. The 1890 census is destroyed and is treated as a gap, not evidence.

4. **Conflicts are resolved, not ignored.** C1 (John D. vs M.) is resolved from the will image itself: John **D.** C2 (father's birthplace Ky vs Va) is resolved for Virginia on the father's own 1860 statement plus the concordant 1900/1910 censuses, the Kentucky readings being later informant hearsay. C3–C5 are age/literacy noise that does not touch any identification. C6 (defective repo will plates) is documented and the true plates supplied. C7 holds every unassigned Cook household unmerged.

## What is NOT concluded

- The White County, Illinois Thomas H. Cook (1880 household; 1918 death naming father George) is a strong candidate for Eliza's son Thomas — supported by the 1873 Adair marriage to Laura M. Vaughan — but his 1880 sheet age (24) conflicts with b. 1844, and the settling Illinois records were outside this question's Adair scope. **Not proven here; proposed as a future question.**
- John D. Cook's whereabouts after 1870 are unknown. Nothing in this question places him anywhere.
- The two unnamed of Eliza's eight children, and the fates of Liddie and Mattie, are not determined by this evidence.
- James Cook (b. ~1920–21) is established as George and Anna's son by the 1930 sheet's relation column; nothing further about him is claimed.
