# Q-2 Analysis — Collateral households of George William Cook, Adair County, 1880–1930

Question: How do the collateral households of George William Cook (1852–1935), Thomas H. Cook, and John D. Cook track across Adair County between 1880 and 1930?

Evidence: five verified census transcriptions (`source-1880-census-ed1.md`, `source-1900-census-ed8.md`, `source-1910-census-ed14.md`, `source-1920-census-ed14.md`, `source-1930-census-ed1-14.md`), the recorded will of Eliza E. Cook (`source-will-eliza-cook.md`), and the logged county-wide Cook/Cooke surveys for each year (log.md, searches 1–10). The 1890 census is destroyed — a coverage gap, never treated as a negative.

## Findings

1. **George William is present in the same district at every enumeration, 1880–1930.** Columbia in 1880 (ED 1; no magisterial district is stated on that sheet), then West Columbia in 1900, 1910, 1920 and 1930 (ED 8 → ED 14 → ED 1-14; Magisterial District 7 is stated on the 1900, 1910 and 1930 sheets — the 1920 sheet gives the precinct and ED 14 without a magisterial district) — first in his mother's household (1880, 1900), then head of his own (1910, alone, five years after Eliza's death — her 1905 death is prior repo work, gen-05 file; 1920 and 1930 with his wife and sons), farming at every appearance (farm schedules cited in 1910 and 1920; "lives on a farm: Yes" in 1930). That is what the censuses prove: **district-level continuity**. That he stayed on the family's Russell's Creek home tract itself is a **probable inference**, not a census fact: it rests on Eliza's will devising him "my interest in the tract of land I now live on" (1900, proved 1906) and on the 1877 Deed Book Z conveyances, which identify the family tract as the Russell's Creek tract (repo gen-05 file) — records outside the census set. The censuses do not identify parcels: farm schedule numbers are agricultural-schedule sequence numbers, not land identifiers, and the neighbouring households turn over completely between censuses (Vaughn/Shearer → Smith/Williams → Bradley/Richards → Willis/Crawhorn → Mitchell/Murray), so no neighbour correlation ties the households to one parcel. No Q-2 source maps West Columbia / MD 7 or the 1920 Greensburg Road onto the Russell's Creek tract by name; the inference is stated, not smuggled.

2. **Neither brother was in Adair County in any census from 1880 to 1930.** H1 (all three brothers track in District 1/2 throughout) is answered at county level — a broader net than the plan's district framing — and on that evidence it fails for Thomas and John; the null H2 holds for them. Both men are absent already at the first census in scope (June 1880), and their mother's will still named both as beneficiaries of her livestock and money twenty years later (Oct 1900). A will naming a son is evidence he was believed living, or at least not known dead, in 1900; it is not evidence of where he lived. (The brothers' presence in the family's 1870 household, and Thomas's birth about 1844, rest on prior repo work and on the leads in Finding 3 — outside Q-2's evidence set, and flagged as such wherever used.)

3. **Thomas H. — a candidate outside Adair, at lead level only.** Three index-level leads point to White County, Illinois: an 1880 Phillipstown household (Thomas H. Cook with wife Laura M., per a FamilySearch index entry and a tree attachment — the sheet itself was not pulled); an Adair marriage index entry (Thomas H. Cook m. Laura M. Vaughan, 30 Dec 1873, his birth given as 1845); and an Illinois death index entry (Thomas H. Cook, d. 31 Oct 1918, White Co., b. 17 Nov 1844 in Kentucky, father George Cook). None of the three records has been examined as an image in this question; they are leads as logged (log searches 2 and 5), their independence is asserted by their variety, not demonstrated — one linkage runs through a FamilySearch tree attachment — and the 1880 index age for the Illinois man (24, b. ~1856) conflicts with b. 1844. **Held as an unexamined candidate for a future question; no household in this analysis is assigned to Thomas, and no confidence beyond "worth pulling the records" is claimed.**

4. **John D. — no candidate at all.** No John of the right generation appears in Adair in any year, and the only adjacent-county lead (John B. Cook, b. 1859, Green Co., 1910; parents' birthplaces unverified) carries the wrong middle initial and no linking evidence. Prior repo work places John in the family's 1870 household (a record outside Q-2's evidence set); from 1880 onward his whereabouts are simply unknown on this evidence. His absence from Adair is established; nothing more is claimed.

5. **The daughters fade from the record inside the scope.** Liddie (17), Laura (13) and Mattie (9) are in Eliza's 1880 household. By 1900 only Laura B. remains with her mother; by 1910 Laura is gone from Adair under the name Cook and is not with George. Eliza's will names no daughter — although Laura was living in her household the June before the will was written. The documents give no reason; marriage, removal, or death are all consistent with the silence, and none is asserted.

6. **Eliza's "8 children born / 4 living" (1900) against the named children.** Six children are named across the records in scope: Thomas H., John D., George W., Liddie, Laura B., Mattie. Two of the eight are unaccounted for — most plausibly children who died young and appear in no record reviewed here. Of the six named, George and Laura were certainly living in June 1900 (they are on the sheet); the "4 living" therefore means exactly two of the remaining **six** children — the four named (Thomas, John, Liddie, Mattie) plus the two unaccounted — were also living then, so **at most two** of the four named were. The arithmetic is stated; no identification is made from it. The whole point rests on Eliza's reported 8/4 being accurate — a single reported count from an undetermined informant.

7. **New person: James Cook, second son of George and Anna** (1930, age 9, b. ~1920–21). Not in the 1920 household (not yet born) and not previously in the repo's family structure for George William, which names Russell only. Carried to the knowledge stage for the gen-05 person file.

8. **Corroboration of the 1911 marriage.** The 1930 sheet gives George's age at first marriage as 60 and Anna's as 17 — consistent within a year with the 27 Sep 1911 marriage record (he was 59, turning 60 the next February; she about 17). The marriage record itself is prior repo work (gen-05 file), outside Q-2's evidence set. Anna is identified with the "Emma/Enna Janes" of that record on the gen-05 identification, supported here by her wife's position in George's household in both censuses, in the same district, with ages tracking (24 in Jan 1920; 35 in May 1930; 17 at marriage in 1911 → b. ~1894).

## Classification (three-axis)

All five census schedules and the will book are **original records**. The census information is of **undetermined informant** throughout — no sheet names its informant — and is treated as primary-quality for the bare facts of who was enumerated where on the date, and hearsay-grade for reported items such as parents' birthplaces, which are weighted accordingly (see C2). The relation-to-head column is direct evidence of the household relationships as the enumerator recorded them; the will is direct evidence of Eliza's stated family (three sons) and of the home-tract devise. The brothers' absence is negative evidence, established by the logged county-wide surveys whose coverage is stated search by search — not by silence in a single index.

## Conflicts

### C1 — John D. vs John M. (the will's second son) — RESOLVED: John D.
The repo (gen-05 file, sources README) read "John D."; a Muse retrieval summary of 2026-11-26 read "John M." Resolution was planned against the repo's will plates, but those plates proved defective (see C6), so the will was pulled fresh (M-6): Will Book M, p. 133, Film 469199 image 72. The lead's enlarged reading of the image: "John **D** Cook" — a clear cursive capital D. The same document's "George **W.** Cook" was misread as "M." by the M-6 pass and corrected to W. on the same enlarged reading; this clerk's capitals are confusable in both directions, which is how both errors arose. Because both prior errors were single-reader misreadings of this hand, a second reader was given the enlarged excerpt cold, with no context: it independently read Thomas H., **John D. ("certain")**, and George W. (see `source-will-eliza-cook.md`). The repo's "John D." stands, on two independent readings. Census searches covered both initials in every year, so no household assignment turns on the outcome — no John of the right generation was found under either.

### C2 — George's father's birthplace — RESOLVED for Virginia, on preponderance
The entries split evenly, and no census names its informant; no informant is invented here, and no argument from who "must have" reported is used — George himself was head of household in 1910, 1920 and 1930 and cannot be presumed to have reported consistently. For Virginia: the 1860 census entry for George Sr.'s household (Pittsylvania, Virginia — prior repo work, gen-06/gen-08 files, outside Q-2's evidence set), and George's 1900 (Va/Va) and 1910 (Va/Va) entries. For Kentucky: George's 1880 entry (father "Ky", mother Va), and his 1920 and 1930 entries (Ky/Ky). The weighing rests on two checks that do not depend on informants. First, proximity: the 1860 entry dates from George Sr.'s own lifetime — the only entry in the set not made decades after his death (it is prior repo work, gen-06/gen-08 files, outside Q-2's evidence set) — and 1900 and 1910 agree with it. Second, internal reliability: the 1920 and 1930 entries give Kentucky for **both** parents — including George's mother Eliza, whose own 1880 and 1900 entries both give her birthplace as Virginia — so those two entries conflict with her own entries on the mother's birthplace, and their identical answer on the father's carries no independent weight. The 1880 "Ky" is a single divergent item on a line that gives the mother correctly as Virginia. On that weighing — 1860, 1900 and 1910 concordant, against one single-item divergence (1880) and one internally unreliable pair (1920/1930) — Virginia is the supported reading. It remains a preponderance judgment on conflicting census items, stated as such. No identity in this question depends on the item.

### C3 — Laura B.'s birth year — noted, no action
Age 13 in 1880 (b. ~1866–67) vs "b. Dec 1870, age 29" in 1900. Ordinary census age drift (or a mis-stated month/year in 1900); same household, same relation, no competing Laura. Identity unaffected. The same class of drift runs through the set — Eliza is 51 in 1880 against the May 1828 birth her 1900 sheet gives (she would have been 52) — and is noted wherever it occurs rather than silently harmonised.

### C8 — Anna's age drift — noted, no action
Marriage at 17 in Sep 1911 implies b. ~1893–94, which makes her about 26 in Jan 1920 (sheet: 24) and about 36 in May 1930 (sheet: 35). The 1920 figure is roughly two years low. The Emma/Enna = Anna identification does not rest on the ages alone: she holds the wife's position in George's household in both censuses, in the same district, and her 1930 age at first marriage (17) matches the age at marriage in the 1911 record as carried in the gen-05 file. Drift noted; identity unaffected.

### C4 — Russell's indexed birth year (1917) vs 28 Nov 1916 — no conflict of substance
The sheet ages fit his 28 Nov 1916 birth (prior repo work, gen-04 file; outside Q-2's evidence set) (3 1/12 in Jan 1920; 13 in May 1930). The 1917 figures are indexers' estimates from the ages, not statements on the sheets.

### C5 — Russell "cannot read and write" (1930) while attending school — recorded as written
A 13-year-old marked in school but illiterate is most plausibly enumerator error. Transcribed as written in `source-1930-census-ed1-14.md`; not corrected, not built on.

### C6 — Repo will plates defective — flagged, not fixed here
`Adair_WBM_p133.png` shows book pages 134–135 (probate certificate + unrelated wills), not p. 133; `Adair_WBM_p134.png` is a byte-identical duplicate of the same spread. Neither held the will text that two repo summaries described from them. M-6 plates (`Adair_WBM_p132-133.jpg`, `Adair_WBM_p134-135.jpg`) now supply the true spreads. Deleting or renaming the defective plates is outside this question's blanket approval (deletions outside a question folder); recommended as a separate cleanup.

## Plan deviations (acknowledged)

- **Geography:** the plan framed H1 by District 1/2 (Columbia / Russell's Creek); the evidence was gathered at county level (every Cook/Cooke in Adair, every year), which is broader and is what the negatives rest on. No Q-2 source reconciles the District 1/2 framing with the West Columbia / MD 7 geography the sheets actually use; the substitution is acknowledged rather than papered over.
- **FAN cluster:** the plan's FAN section proposed capturing Sneed and Janes households in the district as tract-continuity evidence. That capture was not performed: once Finding 1 was downgraded to district-level continuity (farm schedules and neighbours cannot carry tract identity), FAN evidence had no conclusion left to support, and the county-wide Cook surveys already exceeded the plan's district net. Recorded here as a deviation with its reason; a future tract-level question would need the FAN work, the deed chain, and a parcel-level source this question does not have.

### C7 — Unassigned Cook households — held, not merged
Each year's survey captured Cook households that are not the targets (Elijah W. & Nancy E. of Cane Valley/White Oak and their descendants; James R.; James M.; George L. of East Columbia; the Columbia Stevenson brothers-in-law George and W. Walls Cook; Melvin & Etta). Several plausibly belong to the wider kin network, but no record in scope links them to George Sr.'s family, and none is merged or assigned. They are preserved in the log entries so a future collateral question starts from them.
