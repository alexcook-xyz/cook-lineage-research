# Q-2 Analysis — Collateral households of George William Cook, Adair County, 1880–1930

Question: How do the collateral households of George William Cook (1852–1935), Thomas H. Cook, and John D. Cook track across Adair County between 1880 and 1930?

Evidence: five verified census transcriptions (`source-1880-census-ed1.md`, `source-1900-census-ed8.md`, `source-1910-census-ed14.md`, `source-1920-census-ed14.md`, `source-1930-census-ed1-14.md`), the recorded will of Eliza E. Cook (`source-will-eliza-cook.md`), and the logged county-wide Cook/Cooke surveys for each year (log.md, searches 1–9). The 1890 census is destroyed — a coverage gap, never treated as a negative.

Coverage behind each "not in Adair": 1880 — complete Adair Cook/Cooke surname index (47 results, 30 Adair) plus dedicated Thomas and John name searches (log search 1–2). 1900 — named searches plus the M-2b completion survey (25 Adair Cook persons; log searches 3–4). 1910 — collection-filtered survey, 23 Adair persons, plus dedicated John (statewide, b. 1840–1860, all 78 scanned) and Laura searches (log searches 5–6). 1920 — collection-filtered survey, 14 Adair persons, plus a dedicated John search (log search 7). 1930 — collection-filtered survey, all 26 results accounted for, 8 Adair persons (log search 8).

## Findings

1. **George William never left the home tract.** He is in the same place in all five censuses — Columbia / West Columbia, Magisterial District 7 — first in his mother's household (1880, 1900), then head of his own on the same ground (1910, alone, five years after Eliza's death; 1920 and 1930 with his wife and sons). The district and precinct labels shift (ED 1 → ED 8 → ED 14 → ED 1-14) but the ground does not: West Columbia / MD 7 throughout, farm schedules in 1910 (49) and 1920 (69), "lives on a farm: Yes" in 1930. This is exactly the continuity the will set up — Eliza devised him "my interest in the tract of land I now live on" (1900, proved 1906) — and it matches the 1877 Deed Book Z conveyances of the Russell's Creek tract shares to and from his brother Thomas (repo gen-05 file).

2. **Neither brother was in Adair County in any census from 1880 to 1930.** H1 (all three brothers track in District 1/2 throughout) is refuted for Thomas and John; the null H2 holds for them. Both men were of age and gone — or dead — by June 1880, four years before the first census in scope and twenty years before their mother's will still named them as living beneficiaries of her livestock and money (Oct 1900). A will naming a son is evidence he was believed living, or at least not known dead, in 1900; it is not evidence of where he lived.

3. **Thomas H. — a strong candidate outside Adair, not proven here.** Three independent leads converge on White County, Illinois: the 1880 Phillipstown household (Thomas H. Cook with wife Laura M. and son James E.); the Adair marriage record (Thomas H. Cook m. Laura M. Vaughan, 30 Dec 1873, his birth given as 1845); and an Illinois death record (Thomas H. Cook, d. 31 Oct 1918, White Co., b. 17 Nov 1844 in Kentucky, father George Cook). Against them stands one conflict: the 1880 sheet gives that man's age as 24 (b. ~1856), a decade off b. 1844. The identification is plausible and probably right, but Q-2's scope is Adair County and the settling records (White Co. 1900/1910 census sheets, the 1918 death certificate image) were not pulled. **Held as an unproven candidate for a future question; no household in this analysis is assigned to Thomas.**

4. **John D. — no candidate at all.** No John of the right generation appears in Adair in any year, and the only adjacent-county lead (John B. Cook, b. 1859, Green Co., 1910; parents' birthplaces unverified) carries the wrong middle initial and no linking evidence. John's whereabouts after the 1870 household are simply unknown on this evidence. His absence from Adair is established; nothing more is claimed.

5. **The daughters fade from the record inside the scope.** Liddie (17), Laura (13) and Mattie (9) are in Eliza's 1880 household. By 1900 only Laura B. remains with her mother; by 1910 Laura is gone from Adair under the name Cook and is not with George. Eliza's will names no daughter — although Laura was living in her household the June before the will was written. The documents give no reason; marriage, removal, or death are all consistent with the silence, and none is asserted.

6. **Eliza's "8 children born / 4 living" (1900) against the named children.** Six children are named across the records in scope: Thomas H., John D., George W., Liddie, Laura B., Mattie. Two of the eight are unaccounted for — most plausibly children who died young and appear in no record reviewed here. Of the six named, George and Laura were certainly living in June 1900 (they are on the sheet); the "4 living" therefore means exactly two of {Thomas, John, Liddie, Mattie} were also living then. The arithmetic is stated; no identification is made from it.

7. **New person: James Cook, second son of George and Anna** (1930, age 9, b. ~1920–21). Not in the 1920 household (not yet born) and not previously in the repo's family structure for George William, which names Russell only. Carried to the knowledge stage for the gen-05 person file.

8. **Corroboration of the 1911 marriage.** The 1930 sheet gives George's age at first marriage as 60 and Anna's as 17 — independently consistent with the 27 Sep 1911 marriage record (he was 59, turning 60 the next February; she about 17). Anna is the "Emma/Enna Janes" of the marriage record: same woman, written "Anna" by both the 1920 and 1930 enumerators, ages tracking (24 in Jan 1920; 35 in May 1930; 17 at marriage in 1911 → b. ~1894).

## Classification (three-axis)

All five census schedules and the will book are **original records**. The census information is primary for household composition and residence on the enumeration date (informants unknown; individual items such as parents' birthplaces are hearsay-grade and are weighted accordingly — see C2). The relation-to-head column is direct evidence of the household relationships as the enumerator recorded them; the will is direct evidence of Eliza's stated family (three sons) and of the home-tract devise. The brothers' absence is negative evidence, established by the logged county-wide surveys whose coverage is stated search by search — not by silence in a single index.

## Conflicts

### C1 — John D. vs John M. (the will's second son) — RESOLVED: John D.
The repo (gen-05 file, sources README) read "John D."; a Muse retrieval summary of 2026-11-26 read "John M." Resolution was planned against the repo's will plates, but those plates proved defective (see C6), so the will was pulled fresh (M-6): Will Book M, p. 133, Film 469199 image 72. The lead's enlarged reading of the image: "John **D** Cook" — a clear cursive capital D. The same document's "George **W.** Cook" was misread as "M." by the M-6 pass and corrected to W. on the same enlarged reading; this clerk's capitals are confusable in both directions, which is how both errors arose. The repo's "John D." stands. Census searches covered both initials in every year, so no household assignment turns on the outcome — no John of the right generation was found under either.

### C2 — George's father's birthplace — RESOLVED in favour of Virginia
1880: father "Ky", mother Va. 1900: Va/Va. 1910: Va/Va. 1920: Ky/Ky. 1930: Ky/Ky. The 1900 and 1910 entries agree with George Sr.'s own 1860 census statement (Pittsylvania, Virginia — proven line research, repo gen-06/gen-08 files) and with Eliza's Virginia birth. The Kentucky entries cluster in 1880 (informant unknown) and in 1920/1930, when the likely informant was Anna — who married into the family in 1911, could not read or write, and never knew her father-in-law (d. ~1873). Weighed as informant hearsay against two concordant earlier censuses and the father's own statement, the Kentucky readings are the errors. No identity in this question depends on the item.

### C3 — Laura B.'s birth year — noted, no action
Age 13 in 1880 (b. ~1866–67) vs "b. Dec 1870, age 29" in 1900. Ordinary census age drift (or a mis-stated month/year in 1900); same household, same relation, no competing Laura. Identity unaffected.

### C4 — Russell's indexed birth year (1917) vs 28 Nov 1916 — no conflict of substance
The sheet ages fit Nov 1916 precisely (3 1/12 in Jan 1920; 13 in May 1930). The 1917 figures are indexers' estimates from the ages, not statements on the sheets.

### C5 — Russell "cannot read and write" (1930) while attending school — recorded as written
A 13-year-old marked in school but illiterate is most plausibly enumerator error. Transcribed as written in `source-1930-census-ed1-14.md`; not corrected, not built on.

### C6 — Repo will plates defective — flagged, not fixed here
`Adair_WBM_p133.png` shows book pages 134–135 (probate certificate + unrelated wills), not p. 133; `Adair_WBM_p134.png` is a byte-identical duplicate of the same spread. Neither held the will text that two repo summaries described from them. M-6 plates (`Adair_WBM_p132-133.jpg`, `Adair_WBM_p134-135.jpg`) now supply the true spreads. Deleting or renaming the defective plates is outside this question's blanket approval (deletions outside a question folder); recommended as a separate cleanup.

### C7 — Unassigned Cook households — held, not merged
Each year's survey captured Cook households that are not the targets (Elijah W. & Nancy E. of Cane Valley/White Oak and their descendants; James R.; James M.; George L. of East Columbia; the Columbia Stevenson brothers-in-law George and W. Walls Cook; Melvin & Etta). Several plausibly belong to the wider kin network, but no record in scope links them to George Sr.'s family, and none is merged or assigned. They are preserved in the log entries so a future collateral question starts from them.
