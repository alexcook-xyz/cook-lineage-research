## Proof Summary: What Is Proven vs. Open

- **Proven Historical Facts:** George Cook Sr. was an established, free white head of household on **Sandy Creek** in Pittsylvania County by 1782 (`7 white souls`), acquired ~100 acres on Sandy Creek from **George Cook Jr. and wife Ruth** in August 1783 (`DB 7:106–107`), obtained a 44-acre state grant on Upper Double Creek in 1785 (`Grants P:455`), and left a will proved **21 September 1835** (`Will Book 1:293`, indexed `"Sarah & his children"`). ## Biographical Overview & Life

**George Cook Sr.** (designated in courthouse indexes as *"the Testator"*, dying by 21 September 1835) lived along **Sandy Creek**, a tributary of the Dan River in southern [Pittsylvania County, Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsylvania_County,_Virginia "Virginia's largest county by land area, formed in 1767 from Halifax County along the Dan River borderlands with its seat at Chatham."). Already heading a household of seven white persons in both the 1782 and 1785 Virginia State Enumerations (while initially holding `0` taxable acres in 1782), he entered the land records on **9 August 1783** when **George Cook Junr. and wife Ruth**—whose own 97-acre Sandy Creek parcel had been purchased on **2 March 1780** from **Electious Musick Sr.** (*Deed Book 6:34–35*)—conveyed ~100 acres on Sandy Creek to **George Cook Senr.** for £25 (*Deed Book 7:106–107*, witnessed by Joseph Flippen and Elijah Cook).

Two years later, on **30 March 1785**, Cook patented an additional 44 acres on Upper Double Creek under Treasury Warrant No. 8279 (*Grants P:455–456*). Over the next fifty years he farmed in the Sandy Creek community alongside the Flippin, Pruett, Earp, and Musick families. Notably, **Elexius Musick** had stood directly beside George Cook (Generation 9 candidate) in [Spotsylvania County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spotsylvania_County,_Virginia "Central Virginia county founded in 1721 and named for colonial governor Alexander Spotswood, with its seat at Spotsylvania Courthouse.") Court in **June 1748**, pointing toward a shared Spotsylvania-to-Pittsylvania migration stream.

George Cook Sr. died before **21 September 1835**, when his will (*Will Book 1:293*) was proved in Pittsylvania Court. Because no named executor qualified or completed administration, **Thomas H. Wooding**, late Sheriff of Pittsylvania County and administrator *cum testamento annexo*, sold the testator's **97-acre** Sandy Creek tract bounded by Thomas Flippin to John Williams on **27 January 1837** for $107.67 *"according to the Will of the said Cook"* (*Deed Book 39:319–320*).
