## Biographical Overview & Life

**George Cook** (designated in historical deed indexes and legal cross-references as *"George B"*, dying before 15 October 1831) lived during the transformative post-Revolutionary and Early National eras in [Pittsylvania County, Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsylvania_County,_Virginia "Virginia's largest county by land area, formed in 1767 from Halifax County along the Dan River borderlands with its seat at Chatham."). Residing in the southern districts of the county near the Dan River and Sandy Creek, Cook was an established planter who accumulated real property, including a substantial 277-acre tract adjoining the plantation of John Carter. 

Cook married Catharine, with whom he had a family of at least seven surviving children: David, James, Elizabeth, John, Washington, Henry, and George W. Cook. He passed away intestate in the early autumn of 1831. Because he left no written will, his estate passed collectively to his widow and children under Virginia's laws of descent and distribution. On 15 October 1831, his widow Catharine joined with their five adult children to convey the 277-acre homestead to James Nance for $140.74, while leaving reserved lines for their two youngest sons, Henry and George, who were legally minors. His intestate estate conveyance and the subsequent 1836 power of attorney executed by his minor sons created the documentary trail that bridges his family to Kentucky.

## Proof Summary: Proven vs. Open Link Upward

- **Proven (99–100%):** George Cook ("George B") is definitively the father of Generation 6 ([George W. Cook](gen-06-george-w-cook.md)), proven by his 1831 intestate heirs' deed (`DB 33:101`) and his sons' 1836 Kentucky Power of Attorney and follow-up deed (`DB 39:242, 256–257`).
- **Open Link (`~75–80%` Working Hypothesis):** Whether **George Cook Sr. ("the Testator," d. 1835)** is George B's father depends on obtaining the restricted 1835 will (`Pittsylvania Will Book 1, pp. 293–294`) and building the 1782–1837 tax table (Issues #4 & #7). The 27 November 2026 colonial sweep slightly strengthened this bridge indirectly because **Electious Musick Sr.** (who sold 97 acres to George Cooke Jr. in 1780) lived immediately beside our candidate **George Cook** in Spotsylvania County in 1748.
