# [Spotsylvania County, Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spotsylvania_County,_Virginia "Central Virginia county founded in 1721 and named for colonial governor Alexander Spotswood, with its seat at Spotsylvania Courthouse."): Pre-1750 Archival Guide

- **Formation:** Created 1720/1721 from [Essex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essex_County,_Virginia "Colonial Virginia county formed in 1692 from Old Rappahannock County along the Rappahannock River."), [King and Queen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_and_Queen_County,_Virginia "Tidewater/Middle Peninsula Virginia county created in 1691 from New Kent County."), and [King William](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_William_County,_Virginia "Middle Peninsula Virginia county formed in 1702 from King and Queen County along the Pamunkey and Mattaponi Rivers.") counties in the [Colony of Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colony_of_Virginia "First permanent English colony in North America (founded 1607 at Jamestown), governed by the British Crown until 1776.").
- **Parish:** St. George's Parish.
- **Key Pre-1750 Subject:** **George Cook** and his wife **Sarah** (documented 1741–1748).

---

## 1. Relevance to the Lineage

Outside of a 1645 reference in [Warwick County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warwick_County,_Virginia "Extinct colonial Virginia county on the Virginia Peninsula established in 1634 as Warwick River Shire."), Spotsylvania County holds the **only verified pre-1750 `George Cook` landholder in Virginia** prior to the 3 November 1750 [Amelia County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amelia_County,_Virginia "Central Virginia county formed in 1735 from Prince George and Brunswick counties; originally included modern Prince Edward County.") patent (*Patents 29:316–317*):

1. **1741–1742 Land Acquisitions (*Crozier, pp. 158–159*; *Spotsylvania OB, pp. 166–167*):**
   - `15 May 1741 / 4 May 1742`: Bought 100–200 acres from George & Catherine Stubblefield (Harry Beverley tract).
   - `4 May 1742`: Bought 200 acres from John & Anna Sartin.
   - `12 Feb 1742[/43]` (*Patents 21:145–146*): Patented **400 acres** in Spotsylvania by **40 shillings** [treasury right](# "Colonial land patent acquired through a cash payment of 5 shillings per 50 acres, distinct from headright grants based on passenger importation.") (West side of new Mine Bank Road adj. Griffin Fantleroy & Thomas Shelton).
2. **1743–1744 Land Liquidation (*Spotsylvania OB, p. 236*; *Crozier, pp. 164, 168*):**
   - `6 Sep 1743`: **George Cook and Sarah his wife** sold 200 acres to Rev. James Marye and 400 acres to Henry Lewis (`Spotsylvania_OB_p236-237_1743.jpg`).
   - `4 Mar 1744[/45]`: **George Cook and Sarah his wife** sold the final 200-acre Stubblefield tract to Joseph Allen of [Essex County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essex_County,_Virginia "Colonial Virginia county formed in 1692 from Old Rappahannock County along the Rappahannock River.").
3. **7 June 1748 Final Pre-1750 Court Appearance (*Spotsylvania OB, pp. 466–467*):**
   - **George Cook** exhibited a certified public claim for taking up a runaway slave belonging to George Morton (`Spotsylvania_OB_p466-467_1748.jpg`).
   - Crucially, the **very next entry on page 467** is **`Charles Gordon and Elexius Musick`**. **Electious Musick Sr.** later moved south to **Sandy Creek in [Pittsylvania County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsylvania_County,_Virginia "Southside Virginia county formed in 1767 from Halifax County along the Dan and Banister rivers.")**, where he sold **97 acres** to **George Cooke Junior** on 2 March 1780 (*Pittsylvania DB 6:34–35*).

## 2. The Musick–Cook Neighborhood Cluster at Devils Ditch Swamp

A detailed direct audit of *Virginia Land Office Patents No. 21, pp. 145–146* (12 Feb 1742/43) and neighboring patents confirms that George Cook was an immediate geographic neighbor of the Musick family long before their 1748 court appearance and 1780 land transaction:

1. **Shared Property Boundary on Devils Ditch Swamp (1742/43):**
   - In George Cook's 400-acre grant (*Patents 21:145–146*), the survey boundary runs *"South ten Degrees West one hundred and eighty four Poles to a Corner White Oak [at/near] the Fork of Devils Ditch Swamp close by another White Oak hiccory and red Oak a Corner of **George Musick and Nicholas Randalls Land**."*
   - George and Ann Musick had conveyed 170 acres to Nicholas Randall on 3 July 1742 (*Crozier, p. 160*).
   - On 10 July 1745 (*Patents 23:936*), George Musick and John Graves patented 520 acres in the fork of Devils Ditch Swamp adjoining Joseph Roberts.
2. **Edmund Waller Adjoining Patent (1743):**
   - On 25 November 1743 (*Patents 21:584–585*), Edmund Waller received 552 acres in St. George's Parish beginning at Griffin Fantleroy's line *"being a Corner to **George Cooks** Land"* and running to Joseph Roberts's line.
3. **The Beverley Connection and Pre-Spotsylvania Musick Line:**
   - George Musick was taxed for 100 acres on the 1704 [King and Queen County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_and_Queen_County,_Virginia "Tidewater/Middle Peninsula Virginia county created in 1691 from New Kent County.") Quit Rent Roll.
   - On 11 July 1719 (*Patents 10:430*), George Musick of [King William County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_William_County,_Virginia "Middle Peninsula Virginia county formed in 1702 from King and Queen County along the Pamunkey and Mattaponi Rivers.") patented 250 acres in St. John's Parish adjoining **Harry Beverley**.
   - On 28 September 1728 (*Patents 13:382*), Thomas Allen and George Musick patented 1,000 acres in Spotsylvania adjoining Harry Beverley's new line.
   - In 1741/42, George Cook purchased his first Spotsylvania tract from Catherine Stubblefield, daughter of Harry Beverley.

## 3. Why This Link Remains Under Test (~65–70% Probability)

- **For the Match:** Identical husband and wife names (**George Cook & Sarah** in Spotsylvania 1743 and Prince Edward May 1768), [treasury right](# "Colonial land patent acquired through a cash payment of 5 shillings per 50 acres, distinct from headright grants based on passenger importation.") land purchasing, timing (`June 1748` Spotsylvania → `Nov 1750` Amelia), and the shared **Elexius Musick** neighbor tie reaching Sandy Creek in 1780.
- **For Caution:** Between 1777 and 1783, a `George Cook` (with wife **Mary** and witness `Wm. Cook`) bought and sold ~220 acres in Spotsylvania. Because **Spotsylvania Order Book 1749–1755** has only been checked on pages 1–2 and the **General Index to Wills (1722–1947, Film 34055 / DGS 7645804)** is locked, **GitHub Issue #13** tracks verifying whether the 1741–1748 George Cook left Spotsylvania between 1748 and 1750 or stayed concurrently.
