## Transcription
Testimonium (p. 107, lines 12-14): "In Witness whereof the said George Cook [Junor / Senor, see Notes] and Ruth his wife Have hereunto Set there Hand[s] and Seals the Day and year above Written"
Signatures: first "George Cook Jun[r]. L.S." (receipt signature "George Cook Sen[r]." L.S.; body of receipt: "from the within mentioned George Cook Sen[r]. being in full for the Consideration &c"). Both signature lines are written in the same hand as the body; the second signature reads "Sen[r]." in the receipt, which is the clerk's copy of the grantee name, not a separate signer.. 
Witnesses: "Wm. Hedgpeth, Joseph Flippen" / "James Holloway, Elijah Cook, Orlando Smith" (receipt: "Wm. Hedgpeth, Joseph Flipen, James Holloway, Elijah Cook, Orlando Smith").
Court: "At a Court held for Pittsylvania County the 19th Day of August 178[last digit lost] The within Indenture together with the Receipt hereon endorsed was prov[ed] by the Oaths of three of the Witnesses thereto to be the Acts & Deed of the within Named George Cook Jun[r]. and the same was Ordered to be Rec[orded] By the Court Test Will. Tunstall Cl[erk]" Margin: "Exam'd".

## Notes
- Court year: the last digit after "178" is cut off by the trimmed right edge of the IA scan too (enlarged 3x: the 1, 7 and 8 are clear; the next character is only a sliver). Not readable here. Deed text says "eighty three", so 1783 stays an inference; the deed on DB7 p. 88 (leaf 0100 of the same item) is dated 19 Aug 1783 and the court series on neighbouring pages is 1783, which is consistent with 1783 but does not read the digit for this entry.
- Testimonium word: the initial stroke rises to a tall hooked top like the J of "Jun." on lines 3-4 of the same page and unlike the lower, left-curled long-s of "Sen." on the same page; the word ends in "-or". My reading is "Junor" (about 60 percent), with "Senor" the alternative; the earlier reader's "Senor" is not confirmed. Either way the word is the clerk's term for the person named in the deed as grantor ("Junr") if "Junor"; if "Senor" it is a slip. Second reader recommended, ideally on the original or a clean color scan.
- "Flippen" here (p. 107 witness line), earlier reader "Flippin"; medium. "Hedgpeth" (the "gp" form) medium.
