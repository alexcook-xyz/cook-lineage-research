## 1. Status Overview: Why No Ship Can Be Named Yet

Because **Generation 9** (`George Cook`, patented 400 acres in [Amelia County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amelia_County,_Virginia "Central Virginia county formed in 1735 from Prince George and Brunswick counties; originally included modern Prince Edward County.") on 3 November 1750) bought his Virginia land with **40 shillings cash ([treasury right](# "Colonial land patent acquired through a cash payment of 5 shillings per 50 acres, distinct from headright grants based on passenger importation."))** rather than an immigrant **[headright](# "A legal grant of land given to settlers who paid the cost of transporting themselves or another passenger across the Atlantic.")** (`50 acres` per passenger transported across the Atlantic), no surviving ship manifest or port register links directly to him yet. In colonial Virginia, paying cash for frontier land was typical of second- or third-generation Virginians or internal migrants moving from older counties.

**Any commercial online family tree claiming a specific ship (such as the *Mayflower*, *Ark and Dove*, or an arbitrary passenger list) for this Cook lineage is guessing without primary proof.**

## 2. Colonial Virginia & Tidewater Clusters Tested & Ruled Out

Exhaustive sweeps of the **1704 Virginia Quit Rent Rolls** (all 20 surviving counties), **Cavaliers and Pioneers** (Vols. 1–6), **Bristol Parish Register (1720–1789)**, **The Douglas Register**, and surviving county probate/patent records across [Old Rappahannock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Rappahannock_County,_Virginia "Extinct colonial Virginia county existing from 1656 to 1692 along both sides of the Rappahannock River."), [Essex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essex_County,_Virginia "Colonial Virginia county formed in 1692 from Old Rappahannock County along the Rappahannock River."), [King and Queen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_and_Queen_County,_Virginia "Tidewater/Middle Peninsula Virginia county created in 1691 from New Kent County."), [King William](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_William_County,_Virginia "Middle Peninsula Virginia county formed in 1702 from King and Queen County along the Pamunkey and Mattaponi Rivers."), [Caroline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caroline_County,_Virginia "Piedmont Virginia county formed in 1728 from Essex, King and Queen, and King William counties."), [Stafford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stafford_County,_Virginia "Northern Virginia county formed in 1664 along the Potomac and Rappahannock rivers."), [King George](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_George_County,_Virginia "Northern Neck Virginia county formed in 1720 from Richmond County."), and [Orange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange_County,_Virginia "Piedmont and Shenandoah Valley Virginia county created in 1734 from Spotsylvania County.") (`1617–1750`) have cleared the following early lookalikes and negative leads:

1. **1704 Virginia Quit Rent Rolls (All 20 Counties):** Documented 16 distinct `Cook`/`Cooke` freeholders (4,441 acres total)—and **zero `George Cook(e)` landholders** anywhere in the surviving 1704–1705 rolls. (`Geo. Crook` and `Geo. Crooker` in Prince George Co. are distinct surnames; `Rich'd Cook CCC` in Henrico sits inside the `Cock` surname block for Richard Cocke of Charles City.)
2. **1617 Immigrant `Georg Cooke` (*Ship George*, 1617):** Claimed as a [headright](# "A legal grant of land given to settlers who paid the cost of transporting themselves or another passenger across the Atlantic.") by Thomas Davis on **6 March 1633[/4]** in Warwicksqueick (*Patents 1:128*—*not* Capt. Francis Eppes's 1635 Charles City patent as earlier drafts conflated), and documented as a resident landowner in [Warwick County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warwick_County,_Virginia "Extinct colonial Virginia county on the Virginia Peninsula established in 1634 as Warwick River Shire.") by **29 September 1645** (*Patents 2:38*). No known `George Cook` line descends from him into Prince George or Amelia.
3. **Prince George & Bristol Parish (`1703–1789`):** `Richard`, `Robert` (1733 Buckskin Creek → Brunswick `WB 2:145`), `John` (→ Brunswick `WB 2:53`), and `Benjamin` Cook never used the given name `George`.
4. **Blackwater / Surry & Isle of Wight (`William Cooke`, c. 1613–1751):** Four generations (`William I, II, III`, `John`, `Thomas`, `Reuben`) show zero usage of `George`.
5. **Goochland & Cumberland (`John Cooke Sr.`, 1740 Green Creek → 1775 `Cumberland WB 2:193`):** Descended from `Abraham Cook` of New Kent (1704); left sons `James`, `John Jr.`, `Stephen`—no `George`.
6. **Potomac & Rappahannock Basin Negative Checks (`1656–1750`):** Zero pre-1750 `George Cook(e)` or Musick entries in Overwharton Parish (Stafford), St. Paul's Parish (King George/Stafford), or Northern Neck Grants (1690–1775).
7. **Maryland 1776–1778 Neighbor Cluster:** Verified zero `George Cook` households in the 1776 census / 1778 oaths of Prince George's, Frederick, and Montgomery Counties, MD (where Sandy Creek neighbors Pruett, Earp, Hawker, and Butt lived).

## 3. Top Candidate: Spotsylvania County & The Musick Cluster

The primary candidate for Generation 10 is the **George Cook** (wife **Sarah**) documented in [Spotsylvania County, Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spotsylvania_County,_Virginia "Central Virginia county founded in 1721 and named for colonial governor Alexander Spotswood, with its seat at Spotsylvania Courthouse.") (`1741–1748`):

1. **Devils Ditch Swamp Land Bounds (1742/43):** George Cook's 400-acre patent (*Patents 21:145–146*) literally cornered on *"George Musick and Nicholas Randalls Land"* on Devils Ditch Swamp.
2. **Kinship & Neighborhood Continuity:** George Musick's family originated in [King and Queen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_and_Queen_County,_Virginia "Tidewater/Middle Peninsula Virginia county created in 1691 from New Kent County.") (1704) and [King William](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_William_County,_Virginia "Middle Peninsula Virginia county formed in 1702 from King and Queen County along the Pamunkey and Mattaponi Rivers.") (1719) adjoining Harry Beverley—from whose daughter Catherine Stubblefield George Cook purchased land in 1741. In 1748, George Cook and Elexius Musick appeared adjacent to each other in Spotsylvania Court, and in 1780 Elexius Musick sold land to George Cooke Jr. on Sandy Creek in [Pittsylvania County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsylvania_County,_Virginia "Southside Virginia county formed in 1767 from Halifax County along the Dan and Banister rivers.").
