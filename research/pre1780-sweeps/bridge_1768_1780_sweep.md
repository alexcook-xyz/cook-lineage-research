# Pre-1780 Archival Bridge Sweep: Prince Edward County to Pittsylvania County (1754–1785)

**Scope:** Targeted investigation of the **1768–1780** transitional window between Generation 9 (`"George Cook of Prince Edward county"`, grantor of 150 acres on Harris's Creek to Alexander Guill on 18 April 1768, *Prince Edward Deed Book 3*, pp. 366–368) and Generation 8 (`George Cook Sr.`, landless head of 7 white souls in the 1782 Pittsylvania County state enumeration; purchaser of ~100 acres on Sandy Creek from George Cook Jr. and Ruth on 9 August 1783, *Pittsylvania Deed Book 7*, pp. 106–107).

**Evidentiary Standard:** Every finding below explicitly separates **directly inspected primary records / verbatim contemporary transcriptions** from **secondary abstracts, county histories, or user-contributed trees**, and reports **negative sweeps** and **archival access gaps** transparently.

---

## Executive Summary of Findings

1. **No Verified Online Primary Record Places `George Cook(e)` in Prince Edward County After 18 April 1768 — nor in Any Adjacent Corridor County Before 1780:** Across all open online repositories, published state enumerations (1782–1785), Revolutionary Publick Claims indexes, road orders, and newspaper indexes, **no primary source bridges `George Cook` between his 18 April 1768 Prince Edward deed and `George Cook Jr.` / `George Cook Sr.` surfacing in Pittsylvania County in 1780–1782**.
2. **Three High-Value Primary Recoveries & Lookalike Disambiguations:**
   - **Alexander Guill Confirmed on Harris's Creek in November 1774 (*The Virginia Gazette*):** Direct inspection of Purdie & Dixon's *Virginia Gazette* no. 445 (**17 November 1774**) via the Colonial Williamsburg Rockefeller Library Omeka archive (`items/show/148`) recovers a primary stray-horse notice placed by **`ALEXANDER QUILL`** (a clear printer/OCR variant of **Alexander Guill**): *"TAKEN up, near the head of Harris's creek, in Prince Edward, a sorrel mare..."* (Note: search-engine snippets mistakenly attributed this notice to the preceding advertiser, Jacob Magehe of Deep Creek, Cumberland County.) This confirms Alexander Guill remained seated near the headwaters of Harris's Creek six years after buying 150 acres from George Cook.
   - **Bedford (1778) & Campbell (1785) `"George Cook"` Lookalike Resolved as `George Cock/Cocke` (c. 1726–1802):** Direct inspection of Bevin J. Creel's *Bedford County, Virginia Court Orders 1774–1783* (p. 50, Order Book 6, pp. 139–140, **Jan./Feb. 1778 Court**—not March 1774 as secondary snippets misstated) records `George Cook` appointed with William Jones, George Caldwell, and Harry Terrill to appraise the estate of **Isaac Taylor** (whose 20 January 1778 will, *Bedford WB 1:271–272*, was witnessed by `John Cock`). That territory became **Campbell County** in 1782, where Abercrombie & Slatten's *Campbell County Publick Claims* index records **`George Cock` (p. 5)** and **`John Cock` (p. 5)**, while the 1785 Campbell County Personal Property Tax List (transcribed on New River Notes as `Cook, George & 3 sons`, 3 white males 21+, 11 enslaved persons, 7 horses, 22 cattle) captures the same wealthy planter—**George Cock/Cocke** (son of Alexander Cock of Hanover County; will dated 24 December 1801, proved Campbell County 14 February 1802). He is **not** Generation 9 or Generation 8 George Cook (who was already in Pittsylvania County in 1782 and 1785 with 0 enslaved persons).
   - **Pre-1780 Pittsylvania Anchor for `Josias/Josiah Cook` (March–April 1775) & *Virginia Gazette* Disambiguation:** Direct inspection of Cappon & Duff's *Virginia Gazette Index, 1736–1780* (Vol. I, p. 255) reveals two overlooked entries:
     - **`Cook, Josias` (`P28Ap75: 41`):** Alexander Purdie's *Virginia Gazette*, **28 April 1775**, p. 4, col. 1, in which **`Josias Cook of Pittsylvania County`** advertised that Nathaniel, a bound 12-year-old boy, had been stolen from him on **16 March 1775** (corroborated via Paul Heinegg's *Free African Americans* abstract). This places **Josiah Cook** in Pittsylvania County **four years earlier** than his 25 March 1779 Sandy Creek survey (*LVA Grants A:403–404*), while George Cook Sr. and Jr. remain absent from all 1767–1777 Pittsylvania lists.
     - **`Cooke, George` (`PD2Je68: 23`):** Purdie & Dixon's *Virginia Gazette*, **2 June 1768**, p. 2, col. 3—six weeks after the 18 April 1768 Prince Edward deed—corresponds to London parliamentary dispatches covering **George Cooke, M.P. for Middlesex, England** (John Wilkes's rival, who died 5 June 1768), **not** a Virginia resident.
3. **Critical Archival Negative — Revolutionary War Publick Claims:** Direct verification of Abercrombie & Slatten's *Virginia Revolutionary "Publick" Claims* county booklet indexes yields **zero `George Cook(e)` claims** across **Prince Edward, Amelia, Cumberland, Charlotte, Buckingham, Halifax, Bedford, Lunenburg, and Pittsylvania Counties** (and only `George Cock` in Campbell).
4. **Refutation of Online Search-Index Hallucination in `1782 Heads of Families`:** Secondary search-index summaries claimed `George Cook, Senr.`, `George Cook, Jr.`, and `Elijah Cook` appeared on the **1782 Charlotte County** enumeration. Direct inspection of the full digitized text of *Heads of Families at the First Census... Virginia (1782–1785)* proves those entries are solely **Pittsylvania County (1782, p. 41; 1785, p. 100)**; **Charlotte County (1782)** contains zero George, Josiah, Elijah, or Zachariah Cook entries. Likewise, **`Cook, George` on p. 96** (1785) belongs to **Orange County** (List of Benjamin Grymes), not Southside Virginia.

## Task 1: Disposition of the Remaining 250 Acres on Harris's Creek & Prince Edward County Records (1754–1785)

### 1.1 Known Baseline & Boundary Context
- **3 November 1750 (*LVA Patents 29:316–317*):** George Cook patented **400 acres** in Amelia County `"on the head Branches of Harriss's Creek"`, adjoining Hudson's and Williamson's lines.
- **1754:** Prince Edward County was erected from Amelia County (enacted 1753, effective 1754); St. Patrick's Parish was established in 1755 coextensive with Prince Edward County from Nottoway Parish.
- **18 April 1768 (*Prince Edward DB 3:366–368*):** `"George Cook of Prince Edward county"` sold **150 acres** from the southern portion of the 1750 patent (`S 10° W 180 p.`, `W 6° S 120 p.`, `N 3° W 178 p.`, closing `N 86° E 164 p.` by a new division line, adjoining Lockett's and Jones's lines) to **Alexander Guill** for **£13**; acknowledged April Court 1768, **no wife joined / no dower release**.
- **250 Acres Accounted For [SOLVED 2026-11-27]:** On 6 April 1771, George Cook conveyed the remaining 250 acres of his 3 Nov 1750 patent to **Lovel Wilkerson** for 30 pounds (*Prince Edward Deed Book 5*, pp. 67-68). This accounts for 100% of the 400-acre patent (150 ac to Guill in 1768 + 250 ac to Wilkerson in 1771 = 400 ac).
