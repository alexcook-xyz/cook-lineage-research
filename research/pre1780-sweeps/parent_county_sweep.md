# Pre-1754 Colonial Parent-County & Neighbor-Cluster Investigation: Origins of George Cook (1750 Amelia County Patentee)

**Anchor Record (Generation 9):** On **3 November 1750**, **George Cook** received a Crown patent for **400 acres** in **Amelia County** *"on the head Branches of Harriss's Creek"* for **40 shillings** treasury right (*Virginia Land Office Patents No. 29 [1749–1751]*, pp. 316–317, Library of Virginia microfilm reel 27), adjoining *Hudson's Line* and *Williamson's Line*. That tract fell into **Prince Edward County** upon its formation in 1754. On **18 April 1768**, *"George Cook of Prince Edward county"* conveyed **150 acres** on the branches of Harris's Creek—bounded by *Lockett's line* and *Jones's line*—to **Alexander Guill** for £13 (*Prince Edward County Deed Book 3*, pp. 366–368; acknowledged April Court 1768), with **no wife joining or releasing dower**, after which George Cook vanishes from Prince Edward County records.

This investigation tests every surviving pre-1754 Virginia parent-county, sister-county, and Tidewater-origin cluster (`1617–1775`) to determine whether George Cook of Harris's Creek emerged from an established Southside/Piedmont Virginia Cook(e) family, from another Virginia region, or as a newcomer.

> **Evidentiary Standard:** All assertions below distinguish strictly between directly verified primary/published transcriptions and unverified secondary genealogical claims (marked explicitly as *unverified clue*). Archival gaps that could not be inspected in full online are logged explicitly in Section 6.

## Executive Verdict & Origin Hypothesis Scorecard

1. **Complete Negative Sweep Across All Southside & Middle-Piedmont Cook(e) Clusters (`1704–1765`):**
   - **1704 Virginia Quit Rent Rolls:** Zero `George Cook` / `George Cooke` landholders appear in **any** of the 20 surviving 1704/05 counties (verified across both Thomas J. Wertenbaker [1922] and Louis des Cognets Jr. [1958]).
   - **Prince George County & Bristol Parish (`1703–1789`):** Zero `George Cook(e)` entries appear in the 1704 Prince George Quit Rents, surviving `Orders 1714–1720` excerpts, `Wills & Deeds 1713–1728` excerpts, or the complete *Bristol Parish Vestry Book and Register (1720–1789)*. The Bristol Parish Cook cluster (`Richard`, `Robert`, `John`, `Benjamin`) was centered along Nottoway River, Buckskin Creek, and Butterworth Swamp / Cook's Branch in southern Prince George (later Dinwiddie and Brunswick counties), and never used the given name `George`.
   - **Amelia County (`1735–1773`):** Disambiguation of all five contemporary Amelia `Cook(e)` figures (`Joshua`, `Shem`, `John`, `Benjamin`, `Robert`, plus 1773 `James Cooke`) finds **zero** documentary link to **George Cook** of Harris's Creek. Every Amelia Cook(e) in surviving 1736–1738 tithables, 1754–1765 deeds, and *Hening's* 1758 militia pay list belongs to separate clusters (`King William / Orange [Rucker]`, `Flatt Creek [Chisum/Ford]`, `Buckskin Creek / Brunswick`, or `Cumberland [John Cooke Sr.]`), leaving George Cook completely isolated on the western Harris's Creek border.
   - **Goochland / Cumberland / Albemarle / Buckingham (`1728–1775`):** **John Cooke Sr.** (c. 1700–1775, Green Creek / Willis Mountain / Buffalo Creek; *Cumberland WB 2:193*) had three documented sons (`James`, `John Jr.`, `Stephen`) and seven daughters—**no son or brother George**. Neighbor **Thomas Lockett III** of Cumberland County owned adjoining Harris's Creek land (*Patents 29:316–317* / *PE DB 3:366–368*), but surviving evidence shows only geographic adjacency, not kinship.
   - **Tidewater Families (`1617–1750`):** Neither `William Cooke` of Blackwater (Surry / Isle of Wight, 4 generations), nor `Richard Cooke` (Surry / Prince George), nor `Henrico` (`1679` tithables, `1677–1739` Orphans' Court), nor `Charles City` (`1635–1704`) ever produced a `George Cook(e)` line migrating toward Prince George/Amelia. Even the 1617 immigrant **Georg Cooke** (*George*, 1617) was **not** a 1635 Charles City Eppes headright (an earlier draft confusion); he was claimed by Thomas Davis in Warwicksqueick in March 1633/4 (*Cavaliers* 1:17) and held land in **Warwick County** by 29 September 1645 (*Cavaliers* 1:159).

2. **Discovery of the Top Virginia Candidate Lead — `George Cook` (Wife `Sarah`) of St. George's Parish, Spotsylvania County (`1741–1744`):**
   - A systematic sweep of William Armstrong Crozier's *Spotsylvania County, 1721–1800* (1905) uncovered a **George Cook** (and wife **Sarah**) who purchased **100 acres** from George & Catherine Stubblefield (15 May 1741 / rec. 4 May 1742, Crozier p. 158), purchased **200 acres** from John & Anna Sartin (4 May 1742, Crozier p. 159), **patented 400 acres** in St. George's Parish (**12 Feb 1742[/3]**), and then **liquidated all three tracts** between **6 Sept 1743** and **4 March 1744[/5]** (Crozier pp. 164, 168), vanishing from Spotsylvania records for **33 years (`1744–1777`)**.
   - **Chronological Fit:** Selling out in Spotsylvania in March 1744/5 leaves a 6-year window before **George Cook** patented 400 acres on Harris's Creek, Amelia County (**3 Nov 1750**), and the absence of a wife's dower release in the 1768 Prince Edward sale aligns with Sarah either having died or a different man being involved. A `George Cook` with wife **Mary** (and witness `Wm. Cook`) later appears in Spotsylvania in **1777–1783** (Crozier pp. 330, 364, 374)—either the older George returning/remarrying after 1768 or a younger Spotsylvania relative—so Spotsylvania `Patent Book 20/21` (`12 Feb 1742`) and `Order Books (1738–1749)` must be checked before elevating this clue to proof.

## Section 1: Complete 1704 Virginia Quit Rent Roll Audit

Both standard printed editions of the 1704 (and Henrico "Aprill 1705") Virginia Quit Rent Rolls were audited line by line:
- **Thomas J. Wertenbaker**, *The Planters of Colonial Virginia* (Princeton University Press, 1922), pp. 183–247 (`wertenbaker_1922.txt`).
- **Louis des Cognets Jr.**, *English Duplicates of Lost Virginia Records* (Princeton, NJ, 1958), *"The Rent Roll of Virginia, 1704–1705"*, pp. 123–236 (`descognets_1958.txt`).

### Methodological Corrections (Wertenbaker vs. Des Cognets)
1. **Missing Counties in Wertenbaker:** Wertenbaker omitted **Charles City**, **Henrico**, and **Prince George** counties from his 1922 appendix; Des Cognets (1958) prints all **20** surviving rent-roll counties (`pp. 123–236`, excluding the Northern Neck Proprietary counties whose 1704 rolls are not in the Public Record Office series).
2. **Column-Alignment & Page-Break Traps Solved:** In Des Cognets, several counties print two columns of names followed by two columns of acreages, and OCR/index readers frequently misread page breaks:
   - **King & Queen vs. King William:** `Cook, Benjamin (200)`, `Cook, Thomas Jr. (50)`, `Cook, Thomas Sr. (100)`, and `Cook, Jno. (50)` appear in **King & Queen County** (Des Cognets p. 150; Wertenbaker appendix, King & Queen roll), **not** King William County (which begins on Des Cognets p. 157 and has **0** Cook/Cooke entries).
   - **Isle of Wight vs. Nansemond:** `Reubin Cooke (250)` and `Tho. Cook (300)` appear in **Isle of Wight County** (Des Cognets p. 205, 55 names paired to 55 acreages; Wertenbaker p. 195), **not** Nansemond (which has **0** Cook/Cooke entries).
   - **Surry Page-Continuation Duplicate:** `Cook, Eliz'b (200)` is printed at the bottom of Surry p. 210 and repeated at the top of p. 211 as a page-break carryover (Wertenbaker p. 192 shows the same single 200-acre holding).
   - **Henrico `Cook, Rich'd CCC (476)` = Cocke Variant:** On Des Cognets p. 217 (*"A Rent Roll of All the Lands Held in the County of Henrico for Aprill 1705"*), `Cook, Rich'd CCC 476` stands squarely inside the `Cock` surname cluster (`Cock, Wm. 1535`, `Cock, Rich'd Sr. 2180`, `Cock, Cap't Thomas 2976`, `Cock, James 1506`, `Cook, Rich'd CCC 476`, `Cock, Jno. 98`). `"CCC"` denotes **Charles City County** residence—identifying Richard Cocke (Jr.) of Charles City County holding 476 acres in Henrico, recorded with a clerk/transcription `"Cook"` spelling.

## Section 2: Prince George County & Bristol Parish Sweep (`1703–1789`)

Prince George County was formed from Charles City County in **1703**, and in **1735** its western/southern frontier combined with part of Brunswick County to form **Amelia County** (whose western portion became **Prince Edward County** in 1754). Extensive record loss destroyed most colonial Prince George books, leaving three principal surviving corpora: (1) *1704 Quit Rents*, (2) fragmentary *Court Orders (1714–1720)*, *Minute Book (1737–1740)*, and *Wills & Deeds (1713–1728)*, and (3) *The Vestry Book and Register of Bristol Parish, Virginia, 1720–1789* (transcribed by Churchill Gibson Chamberlayne, Richmond, 1898).

### 2.1 Prince George Landholders & Court Records (`1704–1742`)
1. **`James Cooke` (750 ac, 1704 Quit Rents):** Listed at Des Cognets p. 222 (`[48] James Cooke -> 750`). No subsequent link to a `George Cook` has been found in surviving PG or Amelia records.
2. **`William Cooke III` & `Rebecca (Jones)` (`PG Orders 1714–1720`):** Partial *Prince George* transcriptions compiled from LVA records (`Prince-George.pdf`, pp. 31–33) preserve a detinue suit at March Court 1718/19 (`PG Orders 1714–1720`, pp. 291+): **`William Cook and Rebecca his wife agst James Jones`**, concerning an enslaved girl *Judah* (`aged abt 7 years`) and involving **Jane/Jone Cooke**, daughter of **John Cooke**. Secondary Surry/Isle of Wight syntheses (`WmCooke_kueber.pdf.txt`, citing *Prince George Wills & Deeds 1713–1728*, James Jones will 6 Apr / 12 May 1719) identify Rebecca as daughter of James Jones of Prince George and William as **William Cooke III (c. 1654–1740)** of Blackwater, Surry County (*Surry WB 9:248*, 1740). No `George` appears in this family.
3. **False Lead Cleared — Poythress Estate (`PG Wills & Deeds 1713–1728`, p. 33):** *John Poythress's* 1724 will (*PG W&D 1713–1728*, p. 33) mentions `Frank Cook` and `John Cook`. A 1777 Prince George estate division of the Poythress enslaved inventory (`Prince-George.pdf`, pp. 89, 92–93) proves these were **enslaved families** surnamed `Cook` (`"Frank Cook & Family"`, `"Lucy Cook & Family"`), not free white settlers.
4. **`Richard Cooke` & `John Cooke` (`7 Jul 1726` Patents):** Secondary primary-patent abstracts (`WmCooke_kueber.pdf.txt`; `kueber_richard.txt`) cite *Virginia Patent Book 12*, pp. 529 and 531 (**7 July 1726**): **Richard Cooke** patented **198 acres** on the south side of Nottoway River / Stoney Hill Run, and **John Cooke** patented adjacent land on the same date—both in southern Prince George (later Brunswick/Dinwiddie).
5. **`Robert Cooke` (`20 Jun 1733` Patent & `1737–1740` Minute Book):** In *Virginia Patent Book 15* (abstracted in *Magazine of Virginia Genealogy*; `RobtCooke_gt.txt`, L35; search citations place it at p. 38), **20 June 1733**, **Robert Cooke** of Prince George Co. patented **237 acres** of new land on the **north side of Buckskin Creek**, above Thomas Hunke's line and Stark's line (located in what became Dinwiddie County, east of Amelia). In March 1738/9 (`PG Minute Book 1737–1740`, cited via Kueber `kueber_richard.txt`), **`Robert Cooke and John Cooke`** sued Lawrence Higgens and William Crawley. This Robert Cooke moved/fell into **St. Andrew's Parish, Brunswick County**, leaving a will dated **4 Dec 1747**, proved **7 Apr 1748** (*Brunswick WB 2:145*; `southside_virginian.txt`), naming sons `Robert, William, John, Rubin, Nicholas, Frederick, Baryman`, daughters `Martha Hawkins, Sarah Blaxton, Rebeccah Brook`, and brother `Rubin Cooke`.
6. **`Cook's Branch` (`Dec 1742`, *Executive Journals of the Council of Colonial Virginia*, Vol. 5):** On **p. 107–108** (`cookes_branch_ia.txt`), Peter Woodlief petitioned for **800 acres** *"between Cooks Branch & Butterworth Swamp in Prince George County"*. Butterworth Swamp lies in southern Prince George / Dinwiddie County near Hatcher's Run and the Nottoway drainage—within the territory of the Bristol Parish `Cook` cluster and more than 40 miles east of Harris's Creek in western Amelia/Prince Edward.

## Section 3: Amelia County Disambiguation & Tithable/Militia Audit (`1735–1773`)

Amelia County was cut from Prince George and Brunswick in **1735**, stretching westward to the Blue Ridge/Buffalo River boundary until **Prince Edward County** was cut off in **1754**. Surviving early Amelia records (`1736–1738` tithables, *Hening's* `1758` militia pay schedule, and `1754–1773` deeds) reveal **five distinct free `Cook`/`Cooke` men**—plus one enslaved entry—in central/eastern Amelia, **none** of whom are documented as kin to **George Cook** of Harris's Creek on the western border.

### 3.1 Early Amelia Tithables (`1736, 1737, 1738`)
- **`Cook` (sometimes cited as `Mary Cook`, 1736–1737, Below Deep Creek):** On the 1737 Amelia tithable list (`southside_virginian.txt`, L1409, *"A List of Tithables Below Deep Creek"*, Charles Irby's list), *"At Collo. Richd. Randolphs quarters"* lists overseers `Jos. Morton, John Hardin, Ralf Elkon` followed by enslaved tithables: *"Sanco, Warwick, Bellow, Fibb(-), Seasor, Mary, Abram, **Cook**, Driver, Amey, Sue... 14."* Thus `"Cook"` (adjacent to `"Mary"`) at Randolph's quarter was an **enslaved worker**, not a free settler.
- **`Josh. Cook` (`10 Jun 1736`, Between Deep and Flatt Creeks):** Edward Booker's 1736 list records **`Josh. Cook, Charles — 2`**. He is **Joshua Cook**, who later moved south into Brunswick County, appearing in *Executive Journals* (Vol. 5, p. 366) and purchasing land on Peahill Creek in Brunswick County (*Brunswick DB 5:97*, 24 Sep 1751, *unverified clue/secondary index*). He is absent from 1737 and 1738 Amelia tithable abstracts checked.
- **No `George Cook`:** Neither `1736`, `1737` (*Southside Virginian*, Kolbe abstract), nor `1738` Amelia tithable excerpts record any `George Cook(e)`.

### 3.2 Five-Man Disambiguation in Amelia County (`1754–1773`)

1. **`Shem Cooke` (`King William Co. / Raleigh Parish` -> `Granville Co., NC`):**
   - **Origin & Kinship:** Married **Ann Rucker**, daughter of Peter Rucker whose **Orange County will** (dated 18 Jan 1742/3 or 18 Jun 1743; proved 23 Feb 1743/4, *Orange County WB 1:299*, *cited in secondary abstracts*) names son-in-law *Shem Cook* and daughter *Ann*.
   - **Amelia Land & Deeds:** On **26 February 1756** (*Amelia DB 5:402*), David Parrish of **King William County** sold **200 acres** in **Raleigh Parish, Amelia County** to **Shem Cooke** for £40. In **September 1758** (*Hening's Statutes at Large*, Vol. 7, p. 201 [`hening_vol7.txt`, L13541–13578]), **`Shem Cooke`** appears on the **`AMELIA COUNTY`** militia/provisions pay schedule receiving **`5l. 6s.`** alongside **`John Cooke`** (`5l. 6s.`), `William Hudson`, `James Lockett`, and others. On **10 September 1764** (*Amelia DB 9:20*), `Shem Cooke` and `Benjamin Cooke` witnessed a Flatt Creek deed involving `John Cooke`.
   - **Departure:** Secondary/deed abstracts state Shem Cooke sold his Amelia tract on **Hurt's Creek** on **1 October 1765** for £80 (*specific Deed Book/page not directly verified here—marked as unverified citation detail*) and relocated to **Granville County, NC**, where his will (dated 1792, proved August 1796) names wife Ann and sons William, John, Shem Jr., Claibourn, and Rowland.
   - **Relation to George Cook:** None documented. Hurt's Creek and Flatt Creek lie in central/eastern Raleigh Parish (Amelia), whereas George Cook's 1750 patent lay on the far western Appomattox tributaries (Harris's Creek, in Nottoway Parish / 1754 Prince Edward Co.).

2. **`John Cooke` (Wife `Judith`, Flatt Creek, `1754–1765`):**
   - **Land & Deeds:** On **19 November 1754** (*Amelia DB 5:202*; verified verbatim in `Chisum_goyen.txt`, L1096–1105), **John Chisum and wife Eleanor** and **Nicholas Guillintine** sold **200 acres** on **Flatt Creek** in Amelia County to **John Cook** for £100, witnessed by **William Hudson** and **William Southall** *(note: `William Hudson` also appears in Hening 7:201, though Hudsons held multiple Amelia tracts from 1733 onward)*. On **September 1758** (*Hening* 7:201), **`John Cooke`** was paid `5l. 6s.` in the Amelia militia schedule right beside `Shem Cooke`.
   - **1764–1765 Sales:** On **28 February 1764** (*Amelia DB 8:399*; `Cook_ford_lawson.txt`), **John Cooke and wife Judith (X)** sold **34½ acres** in Amelia County to **Christopher Ford**, witnessed by **`Benjamin Cooke`**, Alex. Walker, Claiborne W. Lawson, Wm. Waters, and Thos. Geers. On **10 September 1764** (*DB 9:20*), he transacted on Flatt Creek with witnesses **`Shem Cooke`** and **`Benjamin Cooke`**. On **25 April 1765** (*Amelia DB 8:608*), **John Cooke and wife Judith (X)** joined with **Benedict Hammock Sr.** to sell **205 acres** to **Mary Wainwright of Dinwiddie County** for £60.
   - **Disambiguation:** Distinct from (a) `John Cooke Sr.` of Green Creek / Cumberland County (d. 1775), (b) `John & Dinah Cook` of Bristol Parish / Brunswick (d. 1743), and (c) `John Cook & wife Amy` of Chesterfield County (*Elizabeth City Co. DB E:469*, 24 Jul 1761, sister Deborah married William Chisolm; `Chisum_goyen.txt`, L1225–1229).

3. **`Benjamin Cooke` (1764 Amelia Witness):**
   - Appears in surviving Amelia deed abstracts as a witness on **28 Feb 1764** (*DB 8:399*) and **10 Sep 1764** (*DB 9:20*) for `John Cooke` alongside `Shem Cooke`.
   - **Disambiguation:** Clearly **not** `Benjamin Cook` (1690s–1759/60) of Hanover/Louisa County (*Louisa DB C:44, D½:126, 330*; died by Oct 1760 when son William sold inherited 1725/6 patent land, `Chisum_goyen.txt` L1206–1211 / `genfiles_benj.txt`), and apparently distinct from `Benjamin & Frances Cook` still receiving Bristol Parish disbursements through `1760` (`bristol_parish.txt` pp. 169–175). Probably a close kinsman (son or brother) of `Shem` or `John Cooke` of Raleigh Parish / King & Queen ancestry.

4. **`Robert Cooke` (Bristol Parish -> Brunswick):**
   - As established in Section 2, the 1733 Buckskin Creek (Prince George) patentee fell into **Brunswick County** (will 1747/48, *Brunswick WB 2:145*) and was never an Amelia County Harris's Creek settler.

5. **`James Cooke` (`Amelia DB 12:211`, 1773):**
   - On **31 July 1773** (recorded **24 Feb 1774**, *Amelia DB 12:211*), **James Cooke** executed a £500 bill of sale to **John Ford Jr.** conveying livestock and household goods, including a **`"Black Horse branded GC 7 yrs old"`**.
   - **Disambiguation:** Secondary/Cumberland & Amelia reconstructions identify this man as **James Cooke (c. 1740–1782)**, son of **John Cooke Sr. of Cumberland County** (*WB 2:193*, 1775). James married **Anna Ford** (daughter of Capt. Hezekiah Ford) in September 1764 in Amelia County, purchased 200 acres on Briery Creek in Prince Edward County in late 1764, moved back to Amelia near Ford's Store around 1770, and died c. March–April 1782 with estate settlement in Cumberland County (Nov 1785).
   - **Caveat on `"branded GC"`:** While the 7-year-old black horse's **`GC`** brand (c. 1766) in a 1773 Amelia bill of sale superficially shares initials with `George Cook` (who was still in Prince Edward on Harris's Creek until April 1768), livestock brands frequently belonged to breeders, traders, or previous owners; with **zero** documentary evidence linking James Cooke (a proven son of John Cooke Sr. of Cumberland) to George Cook, the `"GC"` brand can be noted only as an **unverified anomaly/clue**, never as kinship evidence.

*(Note: Two other `John Cook` entries in Hening Vol. 7 [OCR lines 15579 and 16112] were verified in context and belong to **Frederick County** and **Fairfax County** militias, respectively—not Amelia County.)*

## Section 4: Goochland, Cumberland, Albemarle & Buckingham Sweep (`1728–1775`)

Because **Harris's Creek** (near modern Prospect, Prince Edward County) drains into the Appomattox River directly opposite southern **Buckingham County** (cut from Albemarle in 1761; earlier Goochland) and **Cumberland County** (cut from Goochland in 1749), this investigation specifically tested whether **George Cook** spilled southward across the Appomattox from the Upper James / Willis River / Green Creek Cook cluster.

1. **`John Cooke Sr.` (c. 1700–1775) — Green Creek, Willis Mountain & Buffalo Creek:**
   - **Lineage & Patents:** Identified in Bob Juch's primary-record transcripts (`genfiles_abr.txt`, `genfiles_mcal.txt`, `genfiles_cook_index.txt`) and regional syntheses as son of **Abraham Cooke Sr.** (1704 New Kent Quit Rents, 200 ac) and **Martha Clayton** (`Old Rappahannock` -> `St. Paul's Parish, New Kent` -> `Hanover`). On **26 March 1740** (*Virginia Patents 19:937*, cited in secondary abstracts), **John Cooke** patented **400 acres** on **Green Creek** in Goochland County (becoming Cumberland in 1749), followed by **368 acres** on Green Creek in December 1749. Between **1753 and 1774**, a **John Cook(e)** accumulated patents totaling c. 1,425 acres (plus a 285-acre tract disputed with Benjamin Arnold) on the west side of **Willis Mountain** (Albemarle -> **Buckingham County**, formed 1761) and deeds on **Buffalo Creek** (*most Buckingham colonial books burned in 1869, leaving land grants and neighboring Cumberland/Albemarle records as primary witnesses*).
   - **Estate & Heirs:** John Cooke Sr.'s will, dated **27 November 1775** and recorded in ***Cumberland County WB 2:193*** (renounced by widow **Mary**, *verified by secondary citation*), names three sons—**James**, **John Jr.**, and **Stephen**—and seven daughters (including Elizabeth, wife of Col. George Hooper). **No brother or son named `George Cook(e)` appears anywhere in John Cooke Sr.'s patents, Cumberland estate, or Goochland/Cumberland records.**

2. ***The Douglas Register* & Goochland Wills Index Sweep (`1728–1797`):**
   - A full-text search of *The Douglas Register* (`douglasregisterb00doug_djvu.txt`, W. Mac. Jones ed., 1928, including Rev. William Douglas's 1750–1797 parish entries and the *Index of Goochland County Wills*) yielded **19 `Cook`/`Cooke` marriage and birth entries** (`William`, `James`, `Frances`, `Mary`, `Mordecai`, `Elizabeth`, `Edward Nelson`) and **zero `George Cook(e)` entries**.

3. **`Thomas Lockett` & Adjacent Harris's Creek Patentees (`Williamson`, `Hudson`, `Jones`, `Guill`):**
   - **Thomas Williamson** patented **400 acres** on Harris's Creek on **20 August 1747** (*"adj. Lockett"*, secondary patent citation), immediately adjoining George Cook's **3 November 1750** 400-acre patent (*Patents 29:316–317*: *"adj. Hudson's Line and Williamson's Line"*).
   - **Thomas Lockett III** of **Cumberland County** purchased Williamson's Harris's Creek tract; his Cumberland will (dated **28 September 1770**, proved **23 January 1775**, *cited in secondary abstracts*) devised his Prince Edward County land on Harris's Creek to son **Stephen Lockett** and daughter **Martha Bass** (note also an earlier *Thomas Lockett* will in *Goochland WB 5:90*, 1745, verified in *The Douglas Register* Goochland Wills Index, `L30864`).
   - George Cook's **18 April 1768** sale of **150 acres** to **Alexander Guill** (*PE DB 3:366–368*) explicitly runs *"on Lockett's line"* and *"to Jones's line"*, confirming tight neighborhood adjacency on Harris's Creek. However, **no deed, will, witness, or marriage link** connects Thomas Lockett III (or Thomas Williamson, Hudson, or Jones) to George Cook.

## Section 5: Tidewater (`1617–1750`) & Newly Discovered Spotsylvania (`1741–1744`) Evaluation

### 5.1 Re-Audit of Early Tidewater Families (`1617–1750`)
Every major 17th- and early-18th-century Tidewater `Cook(e)` family was tested against *Cavaliers and Pioneers* Vol. 1 (`1623–1666`), Henrico Orphans' Court (`1677–1739`), Henrico `1679` tithables, *Executive Journals* Vol. 5 (`1739–1754`), and primary-cited family reconstructions:

1. **`Georg Cooke` (1617 Immigrant on the *George*) — Correction of the "1635 Charles City" Conflation:**
   - Direct inspection of *Cavaliers and Pioneers*, Vol. 1 (`cavaliers_vol1.txt`) proves **Capt. Francis Eppes's** famous **26 August 1635** Charles City County patent (*PB 1:280*, Nugent p. 30, L5339) **did NOT name `George Cooke`** among its 3 sons and 30 servants.
   - Instead, **Georg Cooke** and Alice Mulleines *"whoe came in the George 1617"* appear as headrights in **Thomas Davis's** **6 March 1633[/4]** patent for 300 acres in **Warwicksqueick** (Isle of Wight area; *PB 1:128*, Nugent p. 17, L3655). Eleven years later, on **29 September 1645** (*PB 2:38*, Nugent p. 159, L21310), **John Williams** patented 250 acres in **Warwick County** bounded *"W. by land of Mr. Tolbott, **S. by George Cookes**, N. by Christopher Boyce."* Thus, the 1617 immigrant was a **Warwick County** resident by 1645—not Charles City—and leaves no known surviving `George Cook` lineage into 18th-century Prince George or Amelia.
2. **`William Cooke` of Blackwater (Surry / Isle of Wight):**
   - Primary-documented chain (`WmCooke_kueber.pdf.txt`): **William Cooke I** (1613–1679; 1664 Blackwater patent *PB 5:399*, 800 ac) -> **William Cooke II** (c. 1633–1698, *IoW WB 2:396*) -> **William Cooke III** (c. 1654–1740, *Surry WB 9:248*, married Rebecca Jones of PG) along with brothers **John** (d. 1711 *Surry WB 6:63*), **Thomas** (d. 1736 IoW), and **Reuben** (d. 1750/1 IoW). Across four generations (`1613–1751`), **not one child was named `George`**.
3. **`Richard Cooke` (Surry / Prince George):**
   - Early `Richard Cooke` headrights appear in *Cavaliers* Vol. 1 (pp. 92, 121, 150, 199; plus 1655 patent *PB 4:10* / Nugent p. 326), feeding the Surry / Bristol Parish `Richard Cook` (`d. post-1731`) cluster (`kueber_richard.txt`). Again, **zero** usage of the given name `George`.
4. **Henrico, Charles City & Brunswick Counties (`1635–1754`):**
   - **Charles City:** Aside from `Adam Cooke, Gent.` (1642 patent near Ward's Creek, *Cavaliers* 1:139), Charles City shows **0 `Cook`/`Cooke`** on the 1704 Quit Rents (only `Cock`).
   - **Henrico:** The surviving **2 June 1679** Henrico tithables list names **Thomas Cocke** (`8 tithables` at Turkey Island) and zero `Cook`; the **1705** Rent Roll shows only `Cook, Rich'd CCC 476` (Richard Cocke of Charles City inside the `Cock` block); and Pauline Pearce Warner's *Henrico County Orphans' Court Book, 1677–1739* (`henrico_orphans.txt`, OCR-scanned) shows no `George Cook(e)`.
   - **Brunswick (`1720–1754`):** *Southside Virginian* will/inventory extracts (`John WB 2:53`, `Robert WB 2:145`, `Henry`, `Abraham`, `James`) and *Executive Journals* Vol. 5 (`Henry` p. 84, `Joshua` p. 366, `John` & `Richard Lot` p. 307, `Pleasant` p. 49 n.) confirm these were Surry/IoW/PG offshoots; **zero `George Cook(e)`** appears.

### 5.2 The Spotsylvania Discovery: `George Cook` & Wife `Sarah` (`1741–1744`)
In sharp contrast to the zero-hit Southside/Piedmont sweep, William Armstrong Crozier's *Virginia County Records, Vol. I: Spotsylvania County, 1721–1800* (New York, 1905, `crozier_spotsylvania.txt`) documents an active **George Cook** in **St. George's Parish, Spotsylvania County** immediately prior to our 1750 Amelia County patent:

1. **15 May 1741** *(recorded 4 May 1742, Deed Book C, Crozier p. 158)*: **George Stubblefield** of St. George's Parish, Spotsylvania Co., and **Catherine**, his wife (daughter and devisee of Harry Beverley, dec'd), sell to **George Cook** of said parish and county, for **£21 10s. curr.**, **100 acres** in Spotsylvania Co. (part of Harry Beverley's patent). No witnesses. *(Note: the 1744 resale below recites 200 acres transferred by Stubblefield & wife in 1742.)*
2. **4 May 1742** *(recorded 4 May 1742, Deed Book C, Crozier p. 159)*: **John Sartin** of St. George's Parish, Spotsylvania Co., and **Anna**, his wife, sell to **George Cook** of the aforesaid parish and county, for **£12 curr.**, **200 acres** in Spotsylvania Co. (part of patent granted to John Sartin, 28 Sep 1728). No witnesses.
3. **12 February 1742[/3]** *(recited in Deed Book D, Crozier pp. 164, 173)*: **George Cook** receives a **400-acre Crown patent** in **St. George's Parish, Spotsylvania County**.
4. **6 September 1743** *(recorded 6 Sep 1743, Deed Book D, Crozier p. 164)*: **George Cook and Sarah, his wife**, of St. George's Parish, Spotsylvania Co., sell to **Revd. James Marye, Clerk**, of the same county, for **£13 curr.**, **200 acres** in St. George's Parish (the Sartin patent tract). Witnesses: Cuthbert Sandys, Cs. Boreman.
5. **6 September 1743** *(recorded 6 Sep 1743, Deed Book D, Crozier p. 164; recited again 7 Oct 1746, p. 173)*: **George Cook and Sarah, his wife**, of St. George's Parish, Spotsylvania Co., sell to **Henry Lewis** of said parish and county, for **£12 curr.**, **400 acres** in St. George's Parish *"granted sd. Cook, by pat., Feby. 12, 1742."* No witnesses.
6. **4 March 1744[/5?]** *(recorded 5 March 1744, Deed Book D, Crozier p. 168)*: **George Cook and Sarah, his wife**, of St. George's Parish, Spotsylvania Co., sell to **Joseph Allen** of South Farnham Parish, Essex Co., for **£47 curr.**, **200 acres** in St. George's Parish (the Harry Beverley / Stubblefield tract *"transferred by sd. Stubblefield and Catherine, his wife, to sd. Cook, in the year 1742"*). Witnesses: James Waggoner, John Evans, Leonard (X) Young.

**Critical Timeline Assessment:**
- Between **September 1743 and March 1744**, **George Cook and wife Sarah** completely liquidated every acre they owned or patented in Spotsylvania County (`200 + 400 + 200 = 800 acres` across the three conveyances).
- After **4 March 1744**, **George Cook disappears entirely from Spotsylvania County for 33 years (`1744–1777`)**. (Crozier p. 173 in Oct 1746 is only Henry Lewis's resale reciting the 1742 patent.)
- Six years after liquidating in Spotsylvania, a **George Cook** patents **400 acres** on Harris's Creek in **Amelia County** on **3 November 1750** (*Patents 29:316–317*)—holding it until **18 April 1768** (*PE DB 3:366–368*), when he sells 150 acres **with no wife joining** (consistent with Sarah having died in the intervening 24 years, or with two distinct men).
- **Important Caution (`1777–1783` Spotsylvania reappearance):** On **31 March 1777** (Crozier p. 330), a `George Cook of Spts. Co.` buys 220 acres from David Woodroof & Richard Owen, and in **1782–1783** **`George Cook and Mary, his wife`** (witnessed by `Wm. Cook`) sell `70 + 40 + 114 = 224 acres` (Crozier pp. 364, 374; deeds later recited 1785–1786 on p. 394). Spotsylvania also held earlier Cooks—`John x Cook & Ann` (`1728` patent, sold 1729–32), `Thomas x Cook & Elizabeth` (`1728` patent, 1746–62), `Edmond x Cook` (`1727`), `Robert Cook` (d. by Nov 1757 adm. bond, Crozier p. 68), and `Daniel Cook` (lawyer of King & Queen Co., 1730)—alongside German churchwarden `Michael Cook` (1733, St. Mark's/Orange). Thus the 1777–1783 Spotsylvania `George Cook` (wife Mary) could be either (a) a younger Spotsylvania kinsman (or unrelated neighbor) OR (b) the former Amelia/Prince Edward patentee returning north and remarrying after selling out on Harris's Creek in 1768 (which simultaneously falls at the right time if his children stayed in Prince Edward / Charlotte / Mecklenburg or moved on). Either way, the **1741–1744 Spotsylvania `George Cook`** is the **only verified pre-1750 Virginia `George Cook` landholder** outside 1617–1645 Warwick County.

## Section 6: Archival Gaps & Priority Next-Step Lookups

To keep every conclusion strictly within verified evidence, the following repositories/volumes were **not** available in full, searchable text during this sweep and remain necessary to confirm or rule out the Spotsylvania and external-migration leads:

1. **Virginia Land Office Patent Books `20` & `21` (`1741–1743`) — LVA Online / Microfilm:** Pull **George Cook's 12 February 1742[/3] 400-acre Spotsylvania patent** verbatim to check his **headrights vs. treasury right (40s.)**, prior county of residence (e.g., Essex, King & Queen, Caroline, or Orange), and bounding neighbors (Stubblefield, Sartin, Beverley, Allen).
2. **Spotsylvania County Court Order Books (`1738–1749`) & Caroline County Order Books (`1732–1754`):** Check whether George Cook of St. George's Parish (`1741–1744`) originated in neighboring **Caroline** or **King & Queen** (or **Essex**, given his 1744 grantee *Joseph Allen of South Farnham Par., Essex Co.*), and check for road orders, tithables, or suits showing where he went after March 1744.
3. **Amelia County Tithable Lists (`1739–1771`) & Court Order Books (`1735–1755`) — LVA Microfilm / Gibson Jefferson McConnaughey Abstracts:** Only `1736`, `1737` (Kolbe), and `1738` excerpts were checked. Inspecting the **1746–1754** Amelia tithables (especially *Nottoway Parish* / western Appomattox lists) and the **1755 Prince Edward County tithables** will prove whether George Cook was already seated on Harris's Creek as a tithable before 1750, who lived in his household (`1751–1768`), and whether any `William` or `John` resided with him.
4. **Prince Edward County Court Order Books (`1754–1775`):** Inspect for George Cook's road surveys, jury service, suits, or estate/remittance notices between the 1754 county cut-off and his unexplained disappearance after **18 April 1768** (*PE DB 3:366–368*).
5. **B.B. Weisiger III Full Volumes (*Prince George W&D 1713–28*, *Charles City 1655–1700*, *Henrico Deeds/Wills 1677–1737*) & Nugent *Cavaliers and Pioneers* Vols. 2 & 3 (`1666–1732`):** Volume 3 (`1695–1732`) was access-restricted (`HTTP 401`) on Internet Archive; verify directly at LVA or physical copy for any late-17th/early-18th-century `George Cook(e)` headright or patent omitted from surviving county rolls.
