# From South-Central Kentucky to the Dan River and Harris's Creek: Documentary Proof of the Patrilineal Lineage of Alex H. Cook (b. 1991) through Pittsylvania, Prince Edward, and Amelia Counties, Virginia

**Author:** Alex Cook

---

## Abstract

This study traces and evaluates the patrilineal lineage of **Alex H. Cook** (born 1991, Hardin County, Kentucky) across seven proven generations from South-Central Kentucky back to Pittsylvania County, Virginia, and presents an explicitly bounded working hypothesis for the eighth generation alongside a pre-1780 archival investigation. Applying the Genealogical Proof Standard (GPS), Part I proves Generations 1 through 6 in reverse-chronological sequence from modern personal knowledge, newspaper obituaries, and twentieth-century federal and Kentucky vital records back to **George W. Cook** (born circa 1813, Pittsylvania County, Virginia; died by September 1873, Adair County, Kentucky). Part II audits five recurring Kentucky and twentieth-century archival traps resolved along the way. Part III proves Generation 7 by linking George W. Cook of Adair County to **George Cook ("George B," died before 15 October 1831)** of Pittsylvania County through an unbroken 1831–1836 estate deed and power-of-attorney chain (*Deed Book 33:101*; *Deed Book 39:242, 256–257*). Part IV transparently evaluates the unproven **Generation 7 → 8 working hypothesis (~75–80% probability)** connecting George B to **George Cook Sr. ("the Testator," died by 21 September 1835)**, disambiguates four to five concurrent adult men named George Cook in Pittsylvania County, refutes prior misreadings of Library of Virginia Chancery cause *143-1840-024* and *Accounts Current Book 7:210–211*, and documents nine excluded public-archive lookalikes. Finally, Part V synthesizes negative borderland sweeps and establishes a mathematical metes-and-bounds match connecting the **3 November 1750 Amelia County patent** (*Patents 29:316–317*, 400 acres on Harris's Creek) to the **18 April 1768 Prince Edward County deed** (*Deed Book 3:366–368*, 150 acres), concluding with a prioritized in-person research checklist to close the remaining restricted archival gaps.

## Introduction and Master Lineage Overview

Patrilineal research on a common surname such as *Cook* across Virginia and Kentucky demands two disciplines above all others: direct visual verification of original courthouse instruments rather than reliance on vendor indexes or secondary abstracts, and strict same-name disambiguation before any generational link is claimed. Early working drafts in this research project suffered from both failure modes—accepting corrupt database indexes, confusing microfilm frame offsets, telescoping distinct Pittsylvania men named George Cook into composite biographies, and introducing unverified dates. The present study reconstructs the entire evidentiary record from primary images and verified transcriptions, explicitly quarantining earlier errors and distinguishing at every step between **directly inspected primary images**, **verified verbatim transcriptions**, and **secondary citations carried forward from prior project files**.

## Part I: Reverse-Chronological Proof — Kentucky to Virginia (Generations 1–6)

Every link across Generations 1 through 6 is evaluated against the five elements of the Genealogical Proof Standard (GPS): reasonably exhaustive research, complete *Chicago* / *Evidence Explained* citations, correlation of independent evidence, resolution of conflicting evidence, and a soundly reasoned conclusion.[^1] Earlier workspace drafts containing fabricated vital dates, invented death certificate numbers, and non-existent numerical Adair County deed and will books (`DB 3:116`, `DB 4:214`, `WB G:342`—Adair County uses lettered books `T`, `X`, `Z`, and `M`) have been quarantined and refuted.[^2][^3]

### Generation 1 → Generation 2: Alex H. Cook (b. 1991) to Brian A. Cook (b. 1962)

**Alex H. Cook**, the author of this study, was born on **17 October 1991** in Hardin County, Kentucky, to **Brian A. Cook** (born **12 July 1962**, Campbellsville, Taylor County, Kentucky) and **Kristi S. Prior project notes record Alex's 1991 Kentucky Birth Index entry (naming mother Kristi S. Hall) and Brian A. Cook's 1962 Kentucky Birth Index entry.[^5] Brian A. Cook's service in the Kentucky National Guard during the 1990–1991 Persian Gulf War is preserved in the family's digital archive via screenshots of *The Kentucky National Guard in the Persian Gulf War 1990–1991* (`KYNGinPGW1.pdf`, pages 92 and 99), indexed under `"brian a"`.[^6] Collateral family testimony is independently corroborated by the published 2025 obituary of Brian's brother Michael D. ("Mike") Cook (1958–2025), cited in the 25 November 2026 working paper, which identifies Brian Cook of Campbellsville among the surviving brothers of the sibling set.[^7] No conflicting evidence exists for the Generation 1 → 2 link.

## Part II: Methodological Audit of Modern & Kentucky Archival Traps

Cook` (Emma Janes Cook) | Emma (b. c. 1894) married George William Cook on 27 Sep 1911—nearly **38 years after** George W. Cook Sr. died in 1873 and ~20 years before Emma's birth. ## Part III: The Virginia Bridge — Generation 6 to Generation 7 (Pittsylvania County, 1831–1836) — **PROVEN**

While the 1860 Adair County, Kentucky, federal census captures George W. Cook's own lifetime declaration that he was born in `"Pitsylvania Va."`—corroborated by the independent Adair County census entries of his brothers James Cook (age 56, born `"PITTSYLVANIA VA"`) and Henry Cook (age 50, born `"VIRGINIA"`)—the documentary proof connecting Kentucky back to Virginia rests upon a tight three-instrument deed chain recorded in the Pittsylvania County Clerk's Office between 1831 and 1836.[^39]

### 1. The 1831–1836 Pittsylvania Estate Conveyances

1. **The 15 October 1831 Heirs' Deed (*Deed Book 33, p. 101*):** On **15 October 1831** (recorded **21 November 1831** per the Pittsylvania Grantor Index), **"Catharine Cook Widow of George Cook Decd, David Cook, James Cook, Elizabeth Cook, John Cook, Washington Cook, Henry Cook & George Cook of the County of Pittsylvania"** conveyed a tract of land to **James Nance** of Pittsylvania County for **$140.74** (`"One Hundred & forty Dollars & Seventy four cents current money of Virginia"`).[^40] Direct visual inspection of the original manuscript page resolves an earlier transcription discrepancy: the tract is explicitly written **`"two hundred and Seventy Seven Acres by late survey"`** (**277 acres**), not 227 acres.[^40] Most crucially, although all seven children are named in the body of the indenture, only six grantors—**Catharine** (by mark), **David** (by mark), **Elizabeth** (by mark), **James** (by mark), **Wasington**, and **John Cook**—signed and sealed the deed at the foot of page 101; two blank seal lines remain beneath Washington and John, and the clerk deliberately left a space in the closing attestation clause where **`Henry Cook`** and **`George Cook`** did not execute the instrument.[^40]

**[EMBED PLATE 8: `plate_07_db33_p101.png` — Pittsylvania County Deed Book 33, p. 101: Heirs of George Cook Dec'd to James Nance (15 Oct / 21 Nov 1831)]**

2. **The 2 November 1836 Cumberland County, Kentucky Power of Attorney (*Deed Book 39, p. 242*):** Five years later, on **2 November 1836** (recorded **19 December 1836**), **"George Cook and Henry Cook of the County of Cumberland and State of Kentucky"** executed a power of attorney empowering **"our Brother James Cook of Adair County and State aforesaid"** to make a deed to **James Nance** for the land in Pittsylvania County `"which land decended to us as heirs at law of George Cook Deceased."`[^41] Both brothers signed personally (`George Cook`, `Henry Cook`), and Cumberland County Deputy Clerk James Haggard began his authentication certificate at the bottom of the page.[^41] Noticeably, the 1836 power of attorney introduces a classic **scribal digit drift**: it describes the inherited tract as **`"two hundred and twenty seven acres"`** (**227 acres**) rather than the **277 acres** recorded in the 1831 deed—a routine clerk's transposition (`277` → `227`) when reciting an out-of-state estate from memory.[^41]

3. **The 10 November 1836 Follow-Up Deed (*Deed Book 39, pp. 256–257*):** Acting under that power of attorney, on **10 November 1836** (recorded **19 December 1836**), **"James Cook of Adair County State of Kentucky for George and Henry Cook of the State of Kentucky (Cumberland County)"** conveyed their remaining undivided interests in the deceased father's tract to **James Nance** for **$45**.[^42] The deed states the exact legal reason George and Henry had not signed in 1831: it conveys the same tract *"that said Nance bought some years since of the heirs of George Cook decd ... which courses and distances of said Land is specified in the deed which said Nance had executed to him from the other legatees of said Geo. Cook decd **at which time the said deed was made the aforesaid George & Henry Cook were infants.**"*[^42]

### 2. Correlation & Proof Conclusion (Gen 6 → Gen 7)

Under Virginia law, an "infant" was any person under twenty-one years of age. Because George and Henry Cook were still minors on **15 October 1831** (born after 15 October 1810) and had both reached legal majority by **2 November 1836** (born before 2 November 1815), their birth window is tightly bracketed to **1810–1815**. This matches **George W. Cook** (born c. 1813 in Pittsylvania County, Virginia) and his brother **Henry Cook** (born c. 1810 in Virginia), both of whom resided in adjacent Cumberland and Adair Counties, Kentucky, alongside their elder brother **James Cook** (of Adair County by November 1836; age 56, born Pittsylvania County, Virginia, in the 1860 Adair census).[^39][^42] No competing George Cook of the proper age, sibling group, and Kentucky migration exists in Pittsylvania County records. **George W. Cook (c. 1813–by Sept. 1873) is conclusively proven to be the youngest son of George Cook ("George B," died before 15 October 1831) of Pittsylvania County, Virginia.**

> **Maternity Deliberately Unassigned:**  
> While **Catharine Cook** is proven to be George B's widow in October 1831, no inspected record proves whether she was the biological mother of George W. Cook (born c. 1813) or a later second wife married after July 1814. Under the Genealogical Proof Standard, unproven maternity is left blank pending resolution of Catharine's marriage record.

## Part IV: Generation 7 → 8 Working Hypothesis (~75–80%) & Pittsylvania Same-Name Disambiguation (1767–1837)

### Generation 7 to 8: George B (d. before 15 Oct. 1831) to George Cook Sr. ("the Testator," c. 1740–1835) — **UNPROVEN WORKING HYPOTHESIS (~75–80% PROBABILITY)**

Stepping one generation above George B confronts an evidentiary boundary that must be stated with complete transparency. **No surviving, directly inspected primary document explicitly names George B (d. before 15 Oct. 1831) as a son of George Cook Sr. ("the Testator," d. by 21 Sept. 1835).** Consequently, the Generation 7 → 8 link is presented strictly as an **unproven working hypothesis (estimated ~75–80% documentary probability)** pending in-person inspection of restricted Pittsylvania County probate volumes and print-only marriage abstracts.

#### 1. What Is Directly Documented for George Cook Sr. ("the Testator")

1. **Pre-1785 Landless Presence & 1783 Acquisition:** In the **1782 Virginia state enumeration** of Pittsylvania County, **George Cook Sr.** headed a household of **7 white souls** (`0` black souls) while taxed on **0 acres** in the 1782 Land Tax Book.[^43] On **9 August 1783** (proved **19 August 1783**, *Deed Book 7, pp. 106–107*), **George Cook Jr.** and wife **Ruth** conveyed approximately **100 acres on Sandy Creek of Dan River** adjoining James Holloway to **George Cook Senr.** for **£25**, witnessed by Lemuel Hedgpeth, **Joseph Flippen**, James Holloway, **Elijah Cook**, and Orlando Smith.[^44] On **30 March 1785**, George Cook also patented **44 acres on Upper Double Creek** by Treasury Warrant No. 8279 (surveyed 28 November 1783).[^45] In the **1785 Virginia state enumeration**, **George Cook Sr.** again appears with **7 white souls**, 1 dwelling, and 1 other building.[^43]
2. **Will Index (*Will Book 1, p. 293*):** The Pittsylvania County *General Index No. 1 to Wills, 1767–1949* (p. 86, line 14) records: **`"Cook | George Sen | Sep 21 1835 | W | 1 | 293 | Cook Sarah & his children"`** (also indexed under devisee `Cook Sarah`).[^46] While secondary notes date the will to 19 March 1827 and describe a life estate to widow Sarah followed by sale and division among unnamed children, **the original manuscript of *Will Book 1, pp. 293–294* (FamilySearch Film #007642302) is digitally restricted** and has not yet been inspected directly.[^47]
3. **1837 Sheriff's Estate Sale (*Deed Book 39, pp. 319–320*):** Direct visual inspection of *Deed Book 39, pp. 319–320* confirms that on **27 January 1837** (proved **29 January 1837** by two witnesses, with a third proving the following day), **Thomas H. Wooding**, late Sheriff of Pittsylvania County and **"administrator with the Will annexed of George Cook Senr Decd,"** sold to **John Williams** for **$107.67** (`"one Hundred & seven Dollars & sixty seven cents"`) **"one certain piece or parcel of Land lying and being in the County of Pittsylvania on Sandy creek and Containing ninety seven acres according to the Will of the said Cook which is bounded by the lands of Thomas Flippin & others."**[^48]

#### 2. Why the Hypothesis Reaches ~75–80% — and Why It Is Not Yet Proof

The case connecting **George B** to **George Cook Sr.** rests on three converging pillars:
- **Tract Continuity on Sandy Creek:** The **97-acre** Sandy Creek tract sold under George Cook Sr.'s will in 1837 matches in acreage and watercourse the **97 acres** on a branch of Sandy Creek purchased by **George Cooke Jr.** from Electious Musick Sr. on **2 March 1780** (*Deed Book 6, pp. 34–35*) and conveyed (as ~100 acres) by George Jr. and Ruth to **George Cook Senr.** in **August 1783** (*Deed Book 7, pp. 106–107*).[^44][^48]
- **Eighteenth-Century `Sr.` / `Jr.` Convention & Generational Arithmetic:** In Virginia county practice, `Senr.` and `Junr.` within the same watercourse most often denoted father and son (though occasionally uncle/nephew or unrelated elder/younger namesakes). George Sr. (household of 7 in 1782, likely born c. 1740) fits chronologically as father to George B (whose youngest son was born c. 1813 and who died by 1831).
- **Shared Sandy Creek Neighborhood & Associates:** Both men operated within the same tightly localized Sandy Creek / Flippen / Earp / Pruett neighborhood, and no other older George Cook testator exists in Pittsylvania County to account for George B's presence.

However, **four unresolved evidentiary gaps** keep the link at **~75–80% (unproven)**:
1. Neither *Deed Book 7:106–107* (1783) nor the will index (`WB 1:293`) contains explicit kinship words linking George B to George Sr.
2. The full text of **Will Book 1, pp. 293–294** (Film #007642302) remains uninspected, and any **estate settlement or distribution account** (*Accounts Current* following the January 1837 sheriff's sale) that might show whether George B's heirs in Adair/Cumberland County, Kentucky, received a payout has not yet been retrieved.
3. **George B's own 277-acre acquisition** has not been traced cleanly to George Sr. (the 1815 Solomon S. Seal conveyance, attested only by Rhody Seal's dower certificate in *Deed Book 15:528*, was never recorded in full, and the 1819–1824 deeds of `Catharine al` in *Deed Books 22:327* and *27:91–92* remain unread).[^49]
4. **The Same-Name Structural Tension:** Earlier secondary notes assumed `George B` was identical to `George Cook Jr.` (husband of Ruth Pruett). Direct inspection of Library of Virginia Chancery cause **143-1840-024** (*George Cook v. Nimrod Earp*) undermines that neat merger by revealing that `George Cook Jr. (× Ruth)` fits the **Earp chancery plaintiff**—who died **intestate** in the poorhouse leaving **Sally** as **`"sole heir"`**—just as closely as he fits George B.

#### Refuting the `"17 September 1831 Will"` Error on LVA Chancery `143-1840-024`

In the 25 November 2026 working draft (*Cook_Lineage_Proof_Paper_2026-11-25.md*, note 40), a critical misreading claimed that the Earp chancery plaintiff died in **late 1831** leaving a probated **"17 September 1831 will"** to his daughter Sally Cook, which was cited to prove he could not be the 1835 Testator.[^50] **Direct inspection of LVA Chancery files `143-1840-024` (117 images) and `143-1837-017` (63 images) completely refutes that claim:**[^51]
- On **16 August 1831**, Hezekiah P. Duley deposed that plaintiff George Cook was **`"between seventy and eighty years old"`** (born c. 1751–1761) and blind (`143-1840-024`, image 20).
- The plaintiff was **alive throughout 1832**: he executed an escrow deed to Parham Seymore on **21 May 1832** (`143-1837-017`, images 16–17) and took/received litigation notices and Phillip Cook's deposition through **September 1832** (`143-1840-024`, image 32, sheets 91 & 103).
- He died **intestate** in the Pittsylvania County poorhouse circa **1835–early 1836** (`Joseph Dooly/Dulin` was acting as administrator by **18 July 1836**, `143-1837-017`, image 35).
- A **24 October 1838** supplemental bill (`143-1840-024`, images 5–6) expressly states that George Cook **`"departed this life intestate"`**, leaving **`Sally`** (wife of Joseph Daly/Duley/Dulin) as his **`"sole heir."`**[^51]

### Negative Sweeps Across the Virginia–North Carolina Borderland & Mid-Atlantic (1748–1831)

To test whether George Cook Sr. (b. c. 1740) or his associate **Elijah Cook** (exempted from levies in August 1782, *Order Book 4b:418*; bought 50 acres adjoining George Cook Senr. on Sandy Creek in June 1785, *Deed Book 7:453–454*; dead by 1805 in *Mixed Probate Book 3*) originated in the immediate Dan River borderland or migrated from New Jersey, systematic sweeps were executed across major archival repositories:[^47]

- **Southside Parent Counties & Borderland North Carolina (1748–1777):** Zero records for George Cook or Elijah Cook appear in **Lunenburg County** tithables (1748–1752), **Halifax County Road Orders** (1752–1767—explicitly including road crews from *Burches Creek to Sandy Creek* on 19 Nov. 1756 and 22 Feb. 1760), **Halifax County** pre-1767 patents/deeds, **Pittsylvania County** 1767 tithables (only **Zachariah Cook**—dead by 25 Sept. 1767, *Order Book 1A:17*—and frontier **William Cook**), **1770 Dillard list**, **1777 Oath of Allegiance**, or colonial **Orange (1755), Caswell (1777), Granville, and Rowan/Guilford (1759–1768)** North Carolina tax lists and grants.[^47]
- **Maryland Sandy Creek Neighbor Cluster (1776–1778):** Although Sandy Creek neighbors **Ambrose Cook Hawker**, **William Berry Earp**, **Zachariah Butt**, and **Samuel Pruett II** have thoroughly documented origins in Prince George's, Frederick, and Montgomery Counties, Maryland, **zero `George Cook` or `Elijah Cook` households** appear in the **1776 Maryland Census** or **1778 Oaths of Fidelity** for those counties—indicating the Cook–Pruett and Cook–Earp marriages occurred after arrival in Virginia.[^58]
- **Maidenhead, Hunterdon County, New Jersey (*TAG* 33 & 47–48):** Direct verification of Lewis D. Cook's peer-reviewed monographs confirms a four-generation New Jersey lineage (**Anthony¹ Cook** d. 1671 Kingston, NY → **Henry² Cook** bp. 1671–d. 1723/24 Maidenhead → **William³ Cook Sr.** c. 1691–after 1756 × **Hannah Phillips**) whose thirteen children registered on 3 March 1756 include an **Elijah⁴ Cook (b. 3 March 1741)** who vanishes from New Jersey after 1756.[^59] However, William³ had **no son George**, and **zero primary records bridge Maidenhead to Pittsylvania County, Virginia**.
- **Library of Virginia Chancery & Internet Archive `CR 28` Sweeps:** A statewide LVA Chancery sweep covering all 53 Pittsylvania `Cook*` suits (1789–1869) found **zero** suits naming **Elijah, James, George B, or Catharine Cook**, and **zero** suits involving `Alexander Guill` or `George Cooke` in Prince Edward or Amelia Counties.[^60] Likewise, direct inspection of Internet Archive's Pittsylvania series (which stops at *Deed Book 10B* and excludes *Will Book 1* and *Accounts Current 7*) and **`Court Record Book 28` (`1829–1831`, `pittcocr28`)** confirmed that the Cook court judgments on pages 394–395 (November Court 1830) involve **`Robert Cook`** and **`John Cook`** debt confessions—not George B's estate.[^61]

### The Pre-1780 Amelia / Prince Edward Boundary-Angle Breakthrough

Because pre-1767 Southside and North Carolina borderland searches proved negative, the strongest documented pre-1780 Virginia lead emerges sixty to seventy miles northeast in **Amelia and Prince Edward Counties**, where a **jurisdictional boundary change in 1754** split a single George Cook landholding across two county record sets:

1. **The 3 November 1750 Amelia County Patent (*Virginia Land Office Patents No. 29, pp. 316–317*):** On **3 November 1750**, for **40 shillings**, Governor/Council President **Thomas Lee** granted **George Cook** **400 acres** in **Amelia County** `"on the head Branches of Harriss's Creek"`, adjoining `Hudson's Line` and `Williamson's Line`.[^62] Direct inspection of the Library of Virginia manuscript records the exact survey perimeter: `E 40° S, 160 poles` → `S 7° W, 52 poles` → `WSW, 42 poles` → **`S 10° W, 263 poles` (crossing Harris's Creek)** → **`W 6° S, 120 poles`** → **`N 3° W, 351 poles`** → `E 41° N, 142 poles` to beginning.[^62]

**[EMBED PLATE 16: `plate_14a_amelia_1750_patent.png` — Virginia Land Office Patents No. 29, pp. 316–317: 400 Acres on Harris's Creek, Amelia County to George Cook (3 Nov 1750)]**

2. **The 1754 County Division & 18 April 1768 Prince Edward Deed (*Prince Edward Deed Book 3, pp. 366–368*):** When **Prince Edward County** was carved out of western Amelia County (enacted 1753, effective **1754**), Harris's Creek fell into Prince Edward County. Fourteen years later, on **18 April 1768** (acknowledged at April Court 1768), **`George Cook of Prince Edward county`** conveyed **`150 acres`** on **`"the Branches of Harrisses Creek"`** to **`Alexander Guill`** of the same county for **£13** (livery of seisin executed 18 April 1768; **no wife joined and no dower release was recorded**).[^63] Direct comparison of the 1768 metes and bounds against the 1750 patent proves mathematically that this 150-acre parcel was carved directly from the southern end of the 1750 patent:

| Survey Call | 1750 Amelia Patent (*Patents 29:316–317*, 400 ac) | 1768 Prince Edward Deed (*DB 3:366–368*, 150 ac) | Geometric Relationship |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Southbound Line** | `South 10° West, 263 poles` | `South 10° West, 180 poles` (on Lockett's line) | Identical bearing; southern 180 poles of the 263-pole call |
| **Southern Base** | `West 6° South, 120 poles` | `West 6° South, 120 poles` | **Exact match** in both bearing and distance |
| **Northbound Line** | `North 3° West, 351 poles` | `North 3° West, 178 poles` (on Jones's line) | Identical bearing; southern 178 poles of the 351-pole call |
| **Closing Line** | *(Continues north to Hudson's line)* | **`North 86° East, 164 poles`** (`"by a new division line"`) | New partition line severing the southern 150 acres |

**[EMBED PLATE 17: `plate_14b_pe_1768_deed.png` — Prince Edward County Deed Book 3, pp. 366–368: George Cook to Alexander Guill, 150 Acres on Harris's Creek (18 Apr 1768)]**

3. **Significance & Disambiguation from Josiah Cook (`1780`):** Immediately after the April 1768 sale to Alexander Guill, **George Cook disappears from Prince Edward County records**—roughly a decade before George Cook Sr. and George Cook Jr. surface on Sandy Creek in Pittsylvania County. What became of the remaining 250 acres of the 1750 patent, and whether the 1768 grantor (or his son) migrated southwest to Pittsylvania County, awaits inspection of Prince Edward County tithable lists and *Deed Books 1–5*. Separately, LVA *Grants A, pp. 403–404* confirms that **Josiah Cook** received **261 acres** on the branches of Sandy Creek in Pittsylvania County on **12 June 1780** (surveyed **25 March 1779**, adjoining Francis Mabary, Thomas Boaz, and Dodson, issued on Ancient Composition of 30 shillings sterling, signed by Governor Thomas Jefferson); Josiah appears adjacent to John Cook (100 acres) rather than George or Elijah Cook on the 1782 land tax list, marking him as a distinct collateral or neighbor requiring separate tracking.[^64]

## Part VI: Conclusion and Prioritized In-Person Archival Research Checklist

Across seven documented generations—from **Alex H. Cook** (b. 1991, Hardin County, Kentucky) backward through **Brian A. Cook** (b. 1962), **Arlin Cook** (1935–1991), **Russell Cook** (1916–1982), **George William Cook** (1852–1935), and **George W. Cook** (c. 1813–1873) to **George Cook ("George B," d. before 15 October 1831)**—every parent–child link meets the Genealogical Proof Standard through direct, mutually corroborating courthouse, federal, and vital records. Above Generation 7, the evidence supports a **~75–80% working hypothesis** connecting George B to **George Cook Sr. ("the Testator," d. by 21 September 1835)** on Sandy Creek, while separating both men from **George Cook (the Earp chancery plaintiff, d. intestate c. 1835–1836 leaving Sally as sole heir)** and illuminating a compelling pre-1780 origin trail in **Amelia and Prince Edward Counties (1750–1768)**.

Because the remaining decisive instruments sit behind digital FamilySearch restrictions, print-only monographs, or unindexed county volumes, further progress depends strictly on targeted archival retrieval rather than online index searches:

1. **Pittsylvania County *Will Book 1, pp. 293–294* (FamilySearch Film #007642302 — Restricted):** Inspect in person at a FamilySearch Center or the Pittsylvania County Circuit Court Clerk's Office (Chatham, Virginia) to retrieve the complete 1827/1835 will of **George Cook Senr.**, identify any named children or estate clauses, and check whether George B's heirs appear by name or share.
2. **Pittsylvania County *Mixed Probate Books 3 & 7* and Post-1837 *Accounts Current*:** Examine *Mixed Probate Book 3* (1797–1805, Elijah Cook's estate), *Book 7* (1821–1824, full settlement/division of the 1823 George Cook estate administered by Richard B. Cook), and *Accounts Current* volumes after January 1837 for Sheriff Thomas H. Wooding's distribution of proceeds from the 97-acre Sandy Creek sale (*DB 39:319–320*).
3. **WKU Print-Only Marriage Abstracts & Catharine Cook's 1819–1824 Deeds:** Consult Kathleen Booth Williams, *Marriages of Pittsylvania County, Virginia, 1806–1830* (Special Collections, Western Kentucky University, Bowling Green, KY) alongside Pittsylvania *Deed Books 22:327* and *27:91–92* (`Catharine al`) and *Deed Book 15:528* (Solomon Seal / Rhody Seal, 1815–1816) to clarify Catharine's marriage date and George B's 277-acre title acquisition.
4. **Prince Edward County *Deed Books 1–5*, *Order Books*, and Tithable Lists (1754–1775):** Trace the disposition of the remaining 250 acres of the 1750 Harris's Creek patent (*Patents 29:316–317*) after George Cook's April 1768 sale of 150 acres to Alexander Guill (*DB 3:366–368*), testing whether the 1768 grantor or his household migrated southwest to Sandy Creek in Pittsylvania County.
5. **Local Kentucky Archival Scan Archiving (Completeness Check):** Pull and store digital copies of the remaining uninspected Adair County and federal citations already referenced in prior project notes—specifically Adair *Deed Book T:464–465* (1866), Adair *Deed Book X:115* (1874), the September 1873 Adair heirs' deed, the 1850 Cumberland County and 1900 Adair County census images, and Anna Bell (Sneed) Cook's marriage and vital records (to resolve the collateral *Sneed*/*Hunt* surname note).
