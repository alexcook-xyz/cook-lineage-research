## 1. Plain-English Status: Why No Ship Can Be Named Yet

Because **Generation 9** (`George Cook`, patented 400 acres in Amelia County on 3 November 1750) bought his Virginia land with **40 shillings cash (treasury right)** rather than an immigrant **headright** (`50 acres` per passenger transported across the Atlantic), no surviving ship manifest or port register links directly to him yet. In colonial Virginia, paying cash for frontier land was typical of second- or third-generation Virginians or internal migrants moving from older counties.

**Any commercial online family tree claiming a specific ship (such as the *Mayflower*, *Ark and Dove*, or an arbitrary passenger list) for this Cook lineage is guessing without primary proof.**

## 2. Colonial Virginia & Tidewater Clusters Tested & Ruled Out

Exhaustive sweeps of the **1704 Virginia Quit Rent Rolls** (all 20 surviving counties), **Cavaliers and Pioneers** (Vols. 1–3, 5, 7), **Bristol Parish Register (1720–1789)**, **The Douglas Register**, and surviving county probate/patent records (`1617–1750`) have cleared the following early lookalikes and negative leads:

1. **1704 Virginia Quit Rent Rolls (All 20 Counties):** Documented 16 distinct `Cook`/`Cooke` freeholders (4,441 acres total)—and **zero `George Cook(e)` landholders** anywhere in the surviving 1704–1705 rolls. (`Geo. Crook` and `Geo. Crooker` in Prince George Co. are distinct surnames; `Rich'd Cook CCC` in Henrico sits inside the `Cock` surname block for Richard Cocke of Charles City.)
2. **1617 Immigrant `Georg Cooke` (*Ship George*, 1617):** Claimed as a headright by Thomas Davis on **6 March 1633[/4]** in Warwicksqueick (*Patents 1:128*—*not* Capt. Francis Eppes's 1635 Charles City patent as earlier drafts conflated), and documented as a resident landowner in **Warwick County** by **29 September 1645** (*Patents 2:38*). No known `George Cook` line descends from him into Prince George or Amelia.
3. **Prince George & Bristol Parish (`1703–1789`):** `Richard`, `Robert` (1733 Buckskin Creek → Brunswick `WB 2:145`), `John` (→ Brunswick `WB 2:53`), and `Benjamin` Cook never used the given name `George`.
4. **Blackwater / Surry & Isle of Wight (`William Cooke`, c. 1613–1751):** Four generations (`William I, II, III`, `John`, `Thomas`, `Reuben`) show zero usage of `George`.
5. **Goochland & Cumberland (`John Cooke Sr.`, 1740 Green Creek → 1775 `Cumberland WB 2:193`):** Descended from `Abraham Cook` of New Kent (1704); left sons `James`, `John Jr.`, `Stephen`—no `George`.
6. **Maryland 1776–1778 Neighbor Cluster:** Verified zero `George Cook` households in the 1776 census / 1778 oaths of Prince George's, Frederick, and Montgomery Counties, MD (where Sandy Creek neighbors Pruett, Earp, Hawker, and Butt lived).
