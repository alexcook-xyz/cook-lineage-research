# Analysis: Q-15, George Cook between the 1771 Prince Edward exit and Sandy Creek, 1771–1782

Analyst: evidence-analyst, 2026-11-28. Era skill: `era-early-modern` (1500–1900). Its ceiling allows `proven` for
landholding, taxed men. The limit here is not the era. It is the access gaps listed in the log.

**Questions the evidence is classified against:**
- **Qa (bridge):** Does any record dated 1771–1779 place a George Cook in Pittsylvania, Lunenburg, Halifax, or the NC
  border counties? (H1, H3)
- **Qb (identity):** Is the George Cook who sold land in Prince Edward in 1771 the same man as, or the father of, either
  George Cook on Sandy Creek in 1780–1785? (H1, H2)
- **Qc (relationship):** Was George Cook Sr. of Pittsylvania the father of George Cook Jr.? (H4)

**Not evidence here:** the Muse findings file (`sources/muse-checkpoints/pittsylvania-db6-db7/Pittsylvania_DB6_DB7_Findings.md`),
the Muse Prince Edward checkpoint, the `research/pre1780-sweeps/` notes, the narrative `people/gen-08-*` and `people/gen-09-*`
files, online trees and compiled pages (theheritagelady.com, WikiTree), and agent reports. They are leads. A statement
from them is used below only if a Q-15 source file confirms it against an image or a record. Otherwise it is marked **(lead)**.

### 4b. Clusters
- **PE: George Cook of Prince Edward, Harris's Creek (1768–1771).** Rows 1–2. Repo leads without S- files (gen-09;
  Patents 29:316–317; PE OB 2 for 1760; PE OB 4:109–110) say he patented the land in 1750, was excused from the county
  levy in 1760, and had a wife Sarah in 1768. If confirmed, he was about 50–80 years old in 1780.
- **JR: George Cook(e) Junior of Sandy Creek (1780–1785).** Linked by the tract (DB 6 → DB 7), the suffix, the county,
  and the acreage. Probable. Wife Ruth by 1783 ([[S-pittsylvania-db7-p106-107-cook-jr-to-cook-sr-1783]]).
- **SR: George Cook Senior of Pittsylvania (1782–1783, perhaps 1785).** Household head without land in 1782; acquires
  Jr.'s tract in 1783. Probable within the cluster. Rows 10b and 11 are not attached.

The three clusters are kept **separate**. No `people/P-*.md` files exist (people are in narrative `gen-*.md` files).
The analyst did not create P- files. Proposed rows are in §7.

### 4c. Who are the two Georges? (see C2)
The sources prove only that two George Cooks coexisted as Senior and Junior. Under `era-early-modern`, Jr./Sr. means
the older and younger men of the same name locally, not necessarily father and son.

| Reading | SR (1782–83) is | JR is | Fits Q-15 sources? | Tension |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | the PE George, aged about 50–80 | his son | yes | A household of about 7 whites is large for a man born before about 1729 (counts unverified). If gen-08's 1835 testator "George Cook Sen" is this line, he must be the 1783 JR, renamed after a death. He cannot be the PE man. |
| R2 | a younger George (perhaps the 1835 testator) | a still younger George | yes | The PE George is then absent from Pittsylvania in 1782. No record shows where he was. |
| R3 | an unrelated older George | an unrelated younger George | yes | None, beyond the land transfer from JR to SR. |

No Q-15 record tells R1, R2, and R3 apart. The PE cluster shares **no** wife (PE: Sarah, lead only; JR: Ruth; SR: none
named), no tract, no associate (the PE witnesses do not overlap the Pittsylvania witnesses), and no "late of Prince
Edward" style with either Pittsylvania cluster. The match is **name and area only**. **Identity PE = SR, or PE =
father of JR, is not established.**

**Analyst's second reading (2026-11-28):** a full-resolution crop of the local image. The word has no J descender and
spells out "-nor". The page's "George Cook Junr. L.S." has a long-descender J and a raised r. The analyst read "Senor"; a later reader disagreed (see Resolution).

Three readers: the first searcher and the analyst read "Senor"; a later re-read of the IA leaf read "Junor" at about 60 percent; the orchestrator's own look at the local image saw an ambiguous capital and a "-nor" ending clipped by the scan edge. The grantor is Jr. on every other line, so a slip is likely whichever it is.
**Effect on the conclusion:** none. Do not use the testimonium to argue that Ruth was Sr.'s wife or that Sr. and Jr. are one man.

## 8. What the evidence supports
The Q-15 sources show three separate facts:
- A George Cook lived on Harris's Creek in Prince Edward and sold his home tract in April 1771.
- Electious Musick held Sandy Creek land from at least 1772.
- A George Cooke **Junior**, already "of Pittsylvania", bought part of Musick's land in March 1780 and sold it in 1783
  to a George Cook **Senior**. The two coexisted in 1782–1785.

No record dated 1772–1779 places any George Cook anywhere. No record links the Prince Edward man to either
Pittsylvania George except by name and region. No record states a Sr.–Jr. relationship.

**Proposed grades:** H1 possible; H2 possible (open); H3 unresolved (search not exhaustive); H4 possible.
**Stalled** under R3.
